## 64. Escorts

An escorted ship waits for the players before it leaves, and on the way it moves only while they stay close. Code: `src/HotCargo/Missions/EscortObjective.cs`, `src/HotCargo/Missions/EscortHail.cs`, `src/HotCargo/Missions/EscortMeeting.cs` (the meet step that both escort objectives run), `src/HotCargo/Missions/EscortGuard.cs` (the guard rule that both run on the way). The escort missions are Convoy Duty (requirement 36) and the chain stage missions `ChainEscort` (The Fence's Ledger, Navy Tender), with the objective `HotCargoEscort`, the Mining Escort (requirements 1860 to 1870), with the objective `HotCargoMiningEscort`, and the Blind Courier (requirements 1890 to 1896), with the objective `HotCargoLead`. Vanilla 0.30.5a has no escort objective (`Modes/Career/Missions/Objectives/`).

1850. **Wait for the answer.** After the accept, the escort does not fly its route. It starts only when a player answers its hail with "Ready" (requirement 1852). The ambush (requirement 36) and the arrival check run only after the answer. Escort missions have no time limit, so no clock runs while the escort waits. The objective line reads "Meet <escort>. It comes to you", after the first hail "Answer the hail of <escort> when you are ready", then "Escort <escort> to the jump point".
1851. **Come to the players.** While the escort waits and is live, its AI origin is the meeting spot: on the line from the nearest player ship (significant ships, lowest ship ID on a tie) to the escort, `EscortMeetGap` (300) from both hulls. It comes closer when the player ship's comms circle (vanilla `SensorManager.DetWorldCommsCircle`) would not reach it with `EscortCommsMargin` (50) to spare, so the player can always call it, but never into the player's hull. The spot is at most `EscortMeetRange` (3,000) from the escort's start, so the escort does not chase the players across the system. Past that range, the players come to the escort. An escort in stasis does not move, like every ship far from the players (vanilla Normal AI has no stasis move module).
1852. **The hail.** When the hull of the nearest player ship is within `EscortHailGap` (800) of the escort's hull, the escort hails that ship like a vanilla AI hail (`CareerSimModeManager.AIHailPlayer`: the comms button flashes, a call widget shows). A chat line says "<escort> hails you: ready to leave?". The escort's comms page shows only "We are fueled and loaded. Ready to leave?", the choice "Ready. Lead the way." and the button "Not yet. Hold here.". "Ready" is a shared choice of the domain `escort` (requirement 600 and later): co-op players see hovers and votes, and the pick goes through the shared choice input. "Not yet" only closes the window. The page answers every call while the escort waits, also without a hail.
1853. **No answer.** The escort waits with no time limit. While a player hull stays within `EscortHailGap`, it hails again every `EscortRehailSeconds` (60 s). Reason: a timeout that starts the route brings back the playtest problem (the escort left while the players looked for it). The players can abandon the mission in the mission log.
1854. **Find the escort.** While the escort waits, the mission's map area is a ring of `EscortMarkerRadius` (600) around the escort, not the destination. The objective line focuses the camera on the escort, like before.
1855. **Start from the meeting spot.** On "Ready", the route starts where the escort is: the ambush share counts from there. When the straight line from there to the destination passes a sun center closer than the route's `SunClearance` (requirement 36), the escort first flies back to its start, where the checked route began, and then to the destination. A chat line says "<escort> is under way. Stay close.".

### On the way

Reason for requirements 1881 to 1883: the game freezes every ship far from all player ships (stasis, `SimStasisManager`). Ships that spawn 2,500 or more from every player ship spawn in stasis (`ShipSpawner.DoSpawn`), and stasis ships wake only near a player ship. A player ship a few thousand away still kept the escort live, but the escort is not a player ship, so the raiders on its route stayed frozen. Live raiders did not fire either: vanilla career AI attacks only ships within the radar of the human team (`CareerSimModeManager.AllowAIToAttack`). Before these rules the escort flew alone past the raiders and arrived.

1881. **Stay close.** After "Ready", the escort moves only while the hull of a player ship (significant ships) is within `EscortGuardRadius` (1,500) of its hull. In stasis it has no hull, so its center counts. Alone, it holds where the last player ship left it: the freighter's AI origin and the miner's move order go there, and the miner stops mining, because its waves follow the mined ore (requirement 1863). A line under the objective line counts down its time alone (requirement 1886). When a player ship comes back, the escort goes on. This covers Convoy Duty, Quiet Freight, Navy Tender and every phase of the Mining Escort after the answer. `EscortGuardRadius` stays well below 2,500, so a moving escort is always live.
1882. **Alone, it draws raiders.** While the escort is alone, a clock runs. At `EscortRaidSeconds` (60 s), raiders find it if it is live: `EscortRaidShips` (2, scaled for co-op, requirement 334) built-in ships with `EscortRaidShipTags` (combat), of the mission tier + `EscortRaidTierOffset` (−1, at least 1), jump in `EscortRaidDistanceMin` to `EscortRaidDistanceMax` (900 to 1,300) from it on a random side. Each escort has one hostile allegiance for its raids, with at most `EscortRaidMaxLive` (4) of them live. They fight around it with vanilla Normal AI. Convoy raids carry the convoy raider tag, so they want the freighter (requirement 36). A chat line says "<escort>: raiders on our scope! Where are you?", and the line under the objective reads "Raiders found <escort>: lost in <time>. Get back to it". An escort in stasis draws its raid when it is live again, because raiders that spawn in stasis do not move. A player ship near the escort resets the clock, so a later time alone can draw another raid.
1883. **Lost alone.** When the escort's time alone (requirement 1886) runs out, raiders take it, live or in stasis: it changes to its raiders' allegiance, hostile to the players, and the objective fails. A chat line says "Contact lost with <escort>. Raiders took it while it waited alone." A player ship back in range resets the clock. The clock stops while the players are in another system, like the rest of the objective.
1886. **Time alone, in sight.** An escort's time alone is `LostSeconds` in its objective's rules, else `EscortLostSeconds` (240 s). Each escort sets one for its trip: Convoy Duty and the chain escorts 240 s (a route of about 9,000), the Mining Escort 300 s (the trip, about 3 minutes of mining, and the trip back). A value must be 0 or above `EscortRaidSeconds` and `EscortLostWarnSeconds`; a bad value stops the offer with an error. While the escort is alone:
    - the line under the objective line counts down: "<escort> alone: lost in 3:12. Stay close", after the raid "Raiders found <escort>: lost in <time>. Get back to it";
    - a red ring of `EscortMarkerRadius` (600) follows it, in space and on the map (the doodad `yarden_zamir.poi_escort_alone`, a red copy of the vanilla target area ring, `mod/missions/escort_alone.rules`); it goes when a player ship is back and with the objective (requirement 996);
    - when the clock starts, a red banner and a chat line say "<escort> is alone and waits for you. Lost in 4:00"; `EscortLostWarnSeconds` (60 s) before the end, again "<escort> is still alone. Lost in 1:00".
1884. **Co-op.** The guard rule runs in the sim tick on every machine, and its state is saved with the objective. A raid uses the sim random stream, so it spawns at the same tick and place on every machine. Tests `missions-escort-guard` (also requirement 1885), `missions-lead`, `mechanics-miningescort` and co-op `mp:coop-core` (step `missions.escort-ambush`). Unit tests: `EscortGuardTests` (`MissionModel.GuardStep`, `MissionModel.SecondsLeft`).
1885. **Raiders see the escort.** Raiders of an escort mission (the convoy raider tag `hc_escort_raider`: ambush, chain and guard raids; the Mining Escort waves `hc_mining_raider`) may attack the escort of an accepted escort mission that is in progress, or a ship with the convoy freighter tag `hc_escort_ship` (the convoy and chain freighters, the Mayday victim of requirement 2431), also where no player radar reaches (`Missions/ConvoyTargetPatch.cs`, `EscortRaiderAttackPatch`). Other AI keeps the vanilla rule.

### Mining Escort

Code: `src/HotCargo/Missions/Mechanics/MiningEscortObjective.cs`, `src/HotCargo/Missions/Mechanics/MinerShip.cs`. Rules: `mod/missions/mechanics/sysgen_mechanics.rules` (group `hc_mech_miningescort`), `mod/missions/mechanics/ai_miner.rules`. Numbers: `Mechanics.MiningEscort` tunables.

1860. **Offer.** Trade stations offer it in `Mechanics.MiningEscortChance` (50 %) of the systems (requirement 131). The field is the nearest asteroid field (POI tag `asteroid_field_poi`, POI type `FieldPoi` in the rules: `cosmoteer.poi_asteroid_field`) to the station that has asteroids with ore inside it, no ship hostile to the players inside it, and no part in a sun's heat zone (`Worlds/HeatZone.cs`). The field's size is its POI's world scale: the radius of the vanilla field (1,500), which the game also uses to draw and explore the POI. Hot Cargo keeps field centers `SunClearance` from a sun, so the edge of a field can reach into the heat; a playtest run burned the miner and the escort there. Field pirates and platforms sit within 750 of the center; a hostile wanderer inside the field blocks the offer too. Vanilla tags its ship graveyards (75 to 100 junk wrecks) with `asteroid_field_poi` too; a graveyard is no mining field, so the offer skips POIs of another type. A co-op round that picked a graveyard mined nothing. Test `mechanics-miningescort` checks the pick with a test sun and with a hostile ship (`mech_mine_block`), and that the offer skips the two graveyards of the start system (`mech_mine_skipped_pois`). Vanilla puts pirates or defense platforms in two of three fields (`sysgen_asteroids.rules`), and a civilian miner avoids them. The ore is the most common ore of the field's asteroids, live or in stasis; a tie goes to the lower resource ID. A system without such a field has no offer. The objective spawns the miner when the offer is made, `SpawnDistanceMin` to `SpawnDistanceMax` (500 to 800) from the station center, as a civilian of the station's faction. The miner is the Ore Tender (requirements 1868 to 1870). Its AI `HotCargoMiner` has no modules, so it follows only the objective's orders.
1861. **Goal.** When the miner is first live, the goal is its free room for the ore, at most `OreGoal` (35). Ore that it already holds then does not count. 35 is about 70 s of hyperium mining without help, less for other ores (requirement 1870).
1862. **Work.** The miner meets the players and waits for "Ready" (requirements 1850 to 1854). Then it flies to the field center with vanilla move orders. Within half of the field's radius it starts mining. Every `StepSeconds` (0.5 s) it mines the nearest asteroid with its ore inside the field and out of every heat zone (asteroids drift) like an autonomous miner (requirement 1070: the work spot, `MineDistance`, `MineReach`, at most `SalvageJobs` salvage orders, with the ore as the collect type). Its mining lasers fire on those orders, and its collector beam and crew bring the ore into the hold. An asteroid in stasis gets a move order to its place. Test `mechanics-miningescort` checks mined ore beyond the dropped ore, so a laser that mines nothing fails, and it moves the miner out of its field to check that its next rock is still inside (`mech_mine_away`).
1863. **Waves.** One wave comes for each share of the goal in `WaveShares` (0.25 and 0.65) that the counted ore has reached, once each, so two waves come, also when a big drop passes both shares at once. Shares reached before the mining starts send their waves when it starts. A wave has `ShipsBase` + `ShipsPerTier` × tier ships (1 + 0.15 × tier, scaled for co-op, requirement 334), at most `MaxLiveShips` (5) live, of tier `ShipTierBase` + `ShipTierPerTier` × tier (the sector tier − 1), from the built-in ships with `ShipTags` (combat). They jump in 1,800 to 2,400 from the miner on a random side, on one hostile allegiance, and fight around the miner (vanilla Normal AI).
1864. **Help.** Each step, loose ore of the miner's type within `CollectRadius` (1,500) of its hull gets the vanilla collect order (`ManualResourceTransfer`, as requirement 1068), so its crew fetches it. Ore that players drop there counts like mined ore. This also runs on the way to the field: while such ore is within reach, the miner stops and takes it in, then flies on. The counted ore updates in every phase after the answer.
1865. **Return and pay.** With the goal in the hold, the miner flies to `DockGap` (200) from the station's hull. When it arrives at the station (requirement 1877), the objective completes, and the pay waits at the station (vanilla `ReceiveRewards`). If no asteroid with its ore is left in the field and no loose ore of its type is near, it also flies home, and the job pays in full.
1866. **HUD.** The objective line says "Meet <miner>" or "Answer the hail of <miner>", then "Escort <miner> to the asteroid field", then "<miner> mines <ore>: <count>/<goal>. Drop yours near it to help", then "Escort <miner> back to <station>". While the miner is alone, the guard line shows under it (requirements 1881, 1882). The map ring marks the miner while it waits, the field (its own radius) on the way and while it mines, then the station.
1867. **Failure and pay.** The mission fails when the miner dies. Pay (BALANCE.md, finding 10): 2× standard money like its peer, a station rescue, 3× fame (the waves pay no kill fame, like Claim Jumpers), 2× reputation, contract terms like a convoy (free, trail weight 0.5, rival group `ore_claims`, informant 5 %). It sits next to Claim Jumpers (requirement 137), which stays.
1868. **The Ore Tender.** A civilian miner built from vanilla parts (`MinerShip.Layout`, the shared builder `Core/ShipBuilder.cs` of the scavenger and the thieves): a two-wide corridor spine; at the nose two mining lasers with their 270 degree arcs forward and the manipulator beam (the collector, 360 degrees) on the center line; behind them the control room and the reactor; two storage bays with an airlock and a side thruster on each flank near the nose, where the crew leave to fetch ore; two crew quarters with their doors into the bays; a fire extinguisher on the spine; armor trim; two braking thrusters and six medium thrusters at the tail. Every small thruster touches a room that crew walk, so crew bring it batteries. 35 parts, 12 crew. The design passes the ship editor checks (`Core/ShipCheck.cs`). It cruises at about 105. It is not a built-in ship. Far from the players the game puts it into stasis like any other ship (`SimStasisManager`, `SerializedStasisShip`), and there it does not move or mine.
1869. **Looks.** It wears the paint of its faction's civilian trade ships: the paint scheme of the faction's trade built-in ship with the lowest ID. Its roof pattern is vanilla decals in the three decal colors of that paint: light panels on the side rooms, mid-tone housings on the lasers, dark chevrons down the spine and caution diamonds on the cargo bays. The headless sim loads no decal textures, so there the design goes without its pattern.
1870. **Rate.** In co-op sim runs (2026-11-25) it mined only inside its field, with no ore dropped. It filled the goal of 35 in about 70 s of hyperium, 50 s of sulfur and 25 s of iron. The field's rocks set the pace, not its lasers: hyperium asteroids hold few ore parts (vanilla `AsteroidDensity` 0.08, sulfur 1). Its two bays hold 80 hyperium or 40 iron, so the goal is `OreGoal` (35). The collector beam brings in ore dropped next to it within seconds.

1877. **One arrival rule for delivered ships.** Every mission step that delivers a ship completes on its own when the ship's hull is within `Mechanics.DeliveryRadius` (800) of the target's hull (`Missions/ShipDelivery`). A point target, such as an escort's jump point, has no hull. It covers the Tow Job and the Rescue Beacon tow (`HotCargoTow`), Salvage Rights (`HotCargoSalvageTow`), Repossession (requirement 2380), the convoy escorts (Convoy Duty, Quiet Freight, Navy Tender), the Blind Courier (requirement 1894) and the Mining Escort miner's return (requirement 1865). There is no comms step. The map ring of the target is the area that the hull must reach. Every delivery pays at a station (vanilla `ReceiveRewards`), the Tow Job too. Tests `mechanics-salvage`, `events-tow`, `missions-escort`, `mechanics-miningescort`.

### Blind Courier

Code: `src/HotCargo/Missions/LeadObjective.cs`, `src/HotCargo/Missions/CourierShip.cs`. Rules: `mod/missions/sysgen_hotcargo.rules` (group `hc_group_lead`). Numbers: the `Missions.Lead*` tunables. Story: raiders burned out the courier's navigation core, so it can only follow a transponder. The players lead it to a jump point.

1890. **Offer and route.** Trade stations offer it in 50 % of the systems. The jump point fits the convoy route rules (requirement 36): `EscortRouteShare` of the nearest gate's distance, inside the gate ring, with the route sun clearance. Of the jump points that fit (`MissionModel.RouteCandidates`, every 15 degrees), it takes the one whose straight route passes the most encounters, so the players must lead the courier around them (`Missions/LeadRoute.cs`, `MissionModel.BestEncounterRoute`). The encounters come from the system's real contents: every ship hostile to the players, live or in stasis (bases, field pirates, patrols, bounty targets), weighs `LeadEncounterShipWeight` (1), a hostile station `LeadEncounterStationWeight` (3), and every nebula cell on a grid of `LeadNebulaGridStep` (1,000) weighs `LeadEncounterNebulaWeight` (0.25). Encounters within `LeadEncounterRange` (2,000) of the straight route count. A jump point with a hostile ship within `LeadDestinationClear` (2,000) does not fit. A tie goes to the jump point closer to the direction of the rules POI. The offer text gives a vague mood line by the main danger of the direct route (the kind with the most weight, `MissionModel.MainGroup`), with no counts and no names: raiders "Traffic control flags raider activity along the direct route.", a hostile station "Traffic control warns of a hostile base near the direct route.", nebulae "The direct route crosses rough space.", nothing "The direct route looks quiet, but raiders watch the lanes.". The chat line on "Ready" repeats it. A system without a fitting jump point has no offer. Pay like Convoy Duty (BALANCE.md, finding 10): 2× money, fame and reputation, and a legal contract with trail weight 0.5 (requirement 100).
1891. **The courier.** The objective spawns it when the offer is made, `LeadSpawnDistanceMin` to `LeadSpawnDistanceMax` (500 to 800) from the station center, as a civilian of the station's faction. It is a small courier built from vanilla parts (`CourierShip.Layout`, the shared builder `Core/ShipBuilder.cs`): a one-wide corridor; the control room and a cargo bay beside its front; the reactor and crew quarters beside its back, with the quarters' door into the bay; four small and five medium thrusters. 17 parts, 6 crew, no weapons, shields or armor, so a raid kills it fast. Its AI is the module-less AI of the Ore Tender (`HotCargoMiner`), so it follows only the objective's move orders.
1892. **Follow.** It meets the players and waits for "Ready" (requirements 1850 to 1854). Then it gets a move order to `LeadFollowGap` (250) behind the hull of the nearest player ship (significant ships, lowest ship ID on a tie), on the line from that ship to the courier. The order changes only when that spot moves more than `LeadFollowSlack` (150), so the courier stops where the players stop. The guard rule holds (requirements 1881 to 1886): with no player ship within `EscortGuardRadius`, it holds, draws raiders, and raiders take it after `LostSeconds` (180 s in its rules: a weak ship that follows the players the whole way).
1893. **One ambush.** When its route progress (from where it answered to the jump point) reaches `LeadAmbushAt` (0.5), one raid jumps in `LeadAmbushDistanceMin` to `LeadAmbushDistanceMax` (1,300 to 1,700) ahead of it, toward the jump point. A raid uses the guard raid ships, tier, allegiance and cap (requirement 1882). A chat line says "<courier>: contacts ahead! Raiders on the lane.". The raiders want the courier like the convoy freighter (requirement 36: `ConvoyTargetPatch` also counts escorts that the mod spawns in code) and may attack it outside player radar (requirement 1885). Arrival comes first, so the ambush never comes on the last tick.
1894. **Arrival and pay.** The step completes when the courier arrives at the jump point (requirement 1877; a point, so it has no hull). The pay waits at a station (vanilla `ReceiveRewards`, requirement 2252). The mission fails when the courier dies.
1895. **HUD and map.** The objective line reads "Meet <courier>. It comes to you", after the first hail "Answer the hail of <courier> when you are ready", then "Lead <courier> to the jump point. It follows your nearest ship", with the guard line under it (requirement 1881). The map ring marks the waiting courier (`EscortMarkerRadius`), then the ring around the jump point that its hull must reach. A click on the line shows the courier (requirement 2292). The jump point marker goes when the objective ends (requirement 996).
1896. **Co-op and tests.** The objective runs in the sim tick on every machine, and "Ready" is the shared choice of the domain `escort`. Test `missions-lead`; co-op `mp:coop-core` (step `missions.escort-ambush`: the client answers, and the courier follows on both machines). Unit tests: `MissionModel.FollowSpot`.
1897. **Its own random stream.** The offer group has the tag `hc_own_stream`: it draws from a random stream of its own, made from the system's seed, the group's tags and the sim time (`Missions/OwnRandomStream.cs`), never from `Sim.Rand`, and the state of `Sim.Rand` is saved before the group and put back after it, because code inside the group can still draw from it directly (the courier joining the sim: in the first fix the co-op world still moved). The game generates a system from `Sim.Rand` and keeps using it (trade routes, offers, AI), so a new group that drew from it would move every later roll: other offers, stations, ships and fights, and 5 game tests that depend on the generated world failed. With its own stream, the system is the same as without the group, except for the courier offer, its jump point and the courier. The courier offer's mission ID comes from the same stream, so it moves no other offer's ID either. Every co-op machine draws the same numbers. Any later group with the tag gets the same.
