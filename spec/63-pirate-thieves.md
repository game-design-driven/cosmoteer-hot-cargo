## 63. Pirate thieves

Pirates that want the players' cargo, not their ship. They fight with the other pirates, but aim at storage. When enough player cargo floats near them, they stop shooting, collect it with crew and a Manipulator Beam Emitter (the "funnel"), flee and jump out. Code: `src/HotCargo/Thieves/`. Pure rules: `Thieves/ThievesModel.cs`. Numbers: the `Thieves` tunables section. Requirements 1770 to 1799.

### Ships and side

1770. **Side.** Thieves are pirates. A thief has the player index of its pirate group, so the pirates of the group are its allies (the vanilla team rule), and every Hot Cargo rule that treats a side of no faction as pirates (personalities, calls, the risk model) treats it so. Their names are "Thief N", a number that no ship in the system has.
1771. **AI.** Thieves fly the vanilla pirate AIs, Normal or Wanderer, with two Hot Cargo modules that `mod/thieves/actions.rules` appends last, so they run first. `HcThief` (strategy) collects and flees (requirement 1779). `HcRob` (targeting) aims the guns at cargo (requirement 1776). For a ship that is not a thief, both do nothing.
1772. **Design.** One design per tier of the vanilla tier table (`TierValueMaximums`, 20 tiers in 0.30.5a), built from vanilla parts in code (`ThiefShip`, like the scavenger of requirement 1342). It is a freighter around a corridor spine. The command core has the funnel and a fire extinguisher at the nose, a small control room, 3 crew quarters (turned so that a door faces the spine) and a storage bay. Segments follow down the spine in a cycle of three: weapons (storage bays on both sides, energy weapons outside), power (a reactor and crew quarters), armor (storage bays, a 2-deep armor shell). A corridor across the width, an airlock and thrusters close the tail, so crew reach every thruster. Every design passes the ship editor checks (`Core/ShipCheck.cs`), and the build fails when one does not. A design has the fewest segments (at least 2, so it has a reactor) whose value reaches 0.85 × the tier's value maximum. It uses the smallest of 3 part kits that gets there within 6 segments: 2-row parts with large laser blasters, 3-row parts with large laser blasters, and 4-row parts with ion beams. The biggest kit stops at 12 segments. From tier 12 the designs stay below the target value (tier 20 reaches about a third): the user confirmed on 2026-11-23 that thief ships stop growing at tier 12 on purpose. Limit: the tier 1 thief is worth 64,000, a little above the tier maximum (60,000), because it needs a reactor segment.
1773. **Spawn.** A thief arrives with full crew, power and empty storage bays, its group's AI and AI parameters (or the encounter's, requirement 1781), and the system tier. Its object ID and random seed come from the sim random source.

### Fight

1775. **Fight.** A thief fights like its pirate AI: it picks its targets and moves with it.
1776. **Aim.** A fighting thief aims all its guns at one part of its attack target. Storage that holds resources comes first, then empty storage, then any other part. It never aims at the command part or a reactor, so the players lose cargo rather than their ship. Within a rank the nearest part wins. The current part stays unless another of the same rank is `SwitchFactor` (2) times nearer. The funnel keeps its own targets. A thief without an attack target leaves the aim to the modules below (Disable, the vanilla value targeter).
1777. **Rules for the rank.** A part is storage, a command part or a reactor by its vanilla part categories (`storage`, `command`, `reactor`). A Tech Vault is not storage to the thieves: it ranks as any other part, since they want cargo, never collect tech crates, and leave the tech to the hunters.

### Loot

1778. **Trigger.** Every second, for each group that still fights, the check sums the value of the loose resources within `LootRadius` (2,000) of the hull of any of its live thieves. Only the resources that player parts dropped count: the vanilla player-drop flag (`NuggetFlags.BelongsToPlayer`), which broken player parts and spilled player storage set. Tech crates do not count. The value is the vanilla sell price of each unit (`Funnel.UnitValue`). At `TriggerShare` (0.04) × the vanilla value maximum of the group's tier (tier 1: 2,400), the whole group stops fighting and collects. A chat line says so. Measured at tier 1 against the starter ship: the trigger comes at about half its hull.
1779. **Collect and flee.** A collecting thief drops its attack orders and switches its guns off (the vanilla on/off toggle, as a player does); the funnel stays on. It flies to the nearest loose resource within `LootRadius` that has room in its bays (not tech crates or mission items, any owner), and orders its crew and funnel to fetch every one within the crew's reach (the scavenger's collect step, requirement 1335, with no cooldown). When nothing is left, its bays are full or `CollectSeconds` (60) are over, it flees: it flies straight away from the nearest player ship with its guns off, and after `FleeSeconds` (12) it jumps out (the Diplomacy leave list, the vanilla FTL effect). The other pirates of the group keep fighting.
1780. **Join.** The first time a ship of a pirate group is live in the system, the group rolls `JoinChance` (0.25) once. A pirate group is a side with an allocated player index (100,000 and up), of no faction and not hunters, hostile to the players, whose ship flies the Normal or Wanderer AI: the system's pirate wanderers, ambushes and pass raiders, and event pirates. On a success, 1 thief × the co-op scale (requirement 334) of the system tier arrives 300 to 600 from that ship, with the group's player index, AI and AI parameters.
1781. **Encounter.** On the first visit of a system from sector `MinSector` (2), `EncounterDelaySeconds` (90) after the arrival, while the Pursuit pacer allows the kind `Thieves` (requirements 905, 906): a roll of `EncounterChance` (0.15). On a success, `EncounterMin` to `EncounterMax` (2 to 3) thieves × the co-op scale arrive together, `EncounterDistanceMin` to `EncounterDistanceMax` (3,000 to 5,000) from the players' first ship, with a new pirate player index and the Normal AI. They patrol 800 around the arrival spot and take targets within 6,000 of it.
1782. **End.** A thief that jumps out, or flies out of the live range while it flees (then it leaves the stasis too), escaped with its cargo. A thief that becomes a wreck died: its storage bays drop their cargo where they break, so the players can win the loot back. Its storage bays drop all of their cargo (requirement 1814). A chat line names the value that escaped. Thieves in stasis that do not flee stay. A jump of the players ends every thief and roll of the system.

### Co-op, telemetry and tests

1783. **Co-op.** Every change runs in the sim tick or the AI tick, in sim order, and reads and writes the run state (`RunState.Thieves`: the thieves with group, tier and phase, the rolled groups, the cargo of fleeing thieves, the counts, the encounter time). Rolls use the sim random source. Text is UI only. The co-op step `thieves.collect` (`tests/autotest-mp/coop-combat.txt`) runs a thief group across two machines.
1784. **Telemetry.** `thief.trigger` (group, tier, value, threshold, thieves) when a group stops fighting, and `thief.end` (group, tier, outcome `escaped` or `died`, phase, stolen value) for each thief.
1785. **Tests.** `thieves-basic` (single player): the encounter roll and the pacer, the aim at storage, the trigger, collect, flee and jump out, a thief that joins a pirate group as its ally, and a thief that dies and drops its storage. Unit tests: `ThievesModelTests` (the rank and pick of parts, the trigger sum and threshold).
1786. **Switch.** `Enabled` (default on; off in autotest games, like the Pursuit, requirement 943, so random thieves never change other game tests) turns the joins and the encounters off. Thieves already in the system keep their behavior.
