## 71. Weld

The opposite of the cut (section 70): the player welds a floating object onto their own ship, and the crew join the two. A ship is one grid, and parts turn only in quarter turns, so the object first snaps into the ship's grid. Code: `src/HotCargo/Weld/`. Numbers: the `Weld` tunables section. Requirements 2480 to 2499.

2480. **Tool.** The build window of a human player's own ship has a Weld button next to the Cut button. It works like the other build tools.
2481. **Targets.** A target is a ship within `Range` (40) of the ship's hull that is one of these: another ship that the player commands (an own ship), or a derelict (the game's junk: no owner and no crew; wrecks, broken pieces, a defeated ship without crew, an abandoned station). A ship or station that someone else owns and mans is not a target; the rule is the same for ships and stations. A target must hold only parts of the ship's own part set (so no asteroids), and no contract, the relic or an abandoned-ship event may name it.
2482. **Pick and ghost.** With the Weld tool, the player first picks a target. Each target shows a box in the art of the vanilla selection box (`SelectPartsState`), except a piece that a weld order holds. An own piece or a wreck out of range shows a faint red box. The object under the cursor shows a bold, filled box and "Click: pick this piece". When the object is not a target, it shows a red box, and the cursor names the reason: too far, not yours, not a hull of this kind, or a mission needs it. A click on a target picks it (the vanilla copy sound); a click on another object plays the error sound and shows the reason. Then the picked target shows as a ghost in the ship's grid, in the art of the vanilla part brush (`PartRules.DrawBlueprint`): each of its parts at a tile position and a quarter turn, blue when the place is valid and red when it is not. The cursor names the reason when the place is not valid. The ghost starts at the target's current place, rounded to the nearest tile and quarter turn, and then follows the cursor one tile at a time, with the clicked tile of the piece under the cursor. R turns the ghost a quarter turn. A right click or Esc returns to picking (the vanilla cancel sound). A part that cannot turn (structure, armor, corridors) keeps rotation 0 in the ship, as the game builds it; when such a part is not square, a turn that changes its shape is not valid ("A part of the piece cannot turn this way").
2483. **Valid place.** No ghost tile may cover a tile of the ship's parts or of its design. A tile here is any tile of a part's whole rect, as the design keeps them (`CommonBasePart.GetCells`): a thruster's rect is larger than its physical tile. At least one ghost part must touch a ship part along a side, with both part rules allowing contact there (the contact rule of spec 2462). A left click on a valid place sends the order and returns to picking; on a place that is not valid, it shows the reason and plays an error sound.
2484. **Order.** The order goes through an MP input and follows the vanilla edit flow as a cut does (spec 2475): in blueprint mode it waits for "Make it so" and "Discard changes" drops it; in live mode it starts at once and "Cancel all" cancels it; the sim checks it again with the same rules and refuses it silently when it no longer passes. A ship has at most one weld order; a new one replaces the old one. A right click with the Weld tool, while no piece is picked, cancels the ship's order. Another tool, or closing the build window, drops a picked piece and keeps a running order. When an order ends without a merge, the target keeps the motion of the ship at its point: it floats where it is and does not drift on at the pull speed.
2485. **Pull.** The ship pulls the target into its place: the target moves at up to `PullSpeed` (4) and turns at up to `TurnSpeed` (45 degrees per second) relative to the ship. While the weld runs (the pull and the join), the target and the ship do not collide, so the piece can turn through the hull, and a held piece does not push the ship. At its place it snaps to the exact tile position and then holds there, moving with the ship, until the weld ends. A target that does not reach its place within `PullSeconds` (30) ends the order with a chat line ("The weld could not pull the piece into place").
2486. **Join.** When the target holds at its place (the vanilla structure putdown sound), each edge where a ship part and a target part are in contact is one crew job, as a cut edge (spec 2464): deconstruction priority, one crew member, `EdgeSeconds` (3) of work, working from the ship's side (inside on a walkable tile, from outside otherwise), and work kept when the crew leave. At the merge the clunk of the separation sounds (spec 2474). Weld work is free.
2487. **Merge.** When every joint edge is done, the target becomes part of the ship. Each target part becomes a ship part at its place, built at once, with its health, and the design gains it. The storage of a part itself (a reactor's battery, a Tech Vault, a tank) moves into the same part of the ship, so tech moves with its vault. The other stored resources (cargo in flexible storage) move into the ship's storage by type (the game's storage rules); what does not fit drops next to the ship as loose cargo. Doors follow the game's automatic door rule. The target's crew become the ship's crew, and the crew aboard the target keep their places. Then the target ship leaves the game. Its ship name, roles and ship AI settings go away. The merge checks the place before it changes anything, and checks that each part was built as planned. When a check fails, the merge removes the parts it added, the order ends with a chat line ("The weld failed. The piece stays free.") and an error report, and the target stays a free object.
2488. **Not a loss.** The target leaves the game without the game's lost-ship handling and without Hot Cargo's ship-loss alert: a weld is not a destruction. A live escort or a protected ship is never a target (spec 2481), so no mission fails by a weld.
2489. **Changes during the weld.** When the target or the ship leaves the system, or when the place is no longer valid (a part built in the way), the order ends. A joint edge whose contact ends needs no more work.
2490. **Visible.** The ghost (spec 2482). A pending weld shows its ghost with the other design edits. During the pull, the joint edges show as the amber dashes of a pending cut. During the join, every machine draws the joint edges on the ship in the art of the cut line (spec 2468): a torch glow with a stitch, a spark where crew work, and a dark seam when done.
2491. **Co-op and save.** Orders and cancels go through MP inputs. The pull, the work and the merge run in the sim tick and read and write the run state (`RunState.Weld`). A save and a load keep the orders, the pull and the work; the tick creates the jobs again.
2492. **Scope.** Only a human player's own ship, in its build window. The AI never welds.
2493. **Tunables.** `Enabled` (true), `Range` (40), `PullSpeed` (4), `TurnSpeed` (45), `PullSeconds` (30), `EdgeSeconds` (3).
2494. **Tests.** `weld-own` (a ship cut into two own ships is welded back: pull, join work, merge; health, resources and crew are kept), `weld-wreck` (a derelict piece is welded on; a place that covers a ship tile is refused; in blueprint mode the weld waits for "Make it so"), `weld-turns` (a piece of structure, armor and wedges is welded on at a quarter, a half and three quarter turns), `weld-arm` (joints on the tip of a long armor arm, far from walkable tiles; crew reach them from outside), `weld-cancel` (a right click during the pull, "Cancel all" during the join, a cancel just before the merge and "Discard changes" each end the order, the piece floats free, and a new weld merges; Esc returns from a picked piece to picking), `weld-thrusters` (a cut-off piece with thrusters is welded back at a half turn and merges) and the co-op step `weld.join` in `mp:coop-core`. Unit tests: `WeldModelTests`.
