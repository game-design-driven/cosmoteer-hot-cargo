## 71. Weld

The opposite of the cut (section 70): the player welds a floating object onto their own ship, and the crew join the two. A ship is one grid, and parts turn only in quarter turns, so the object first snaps into the ship's grid. Code: `src/HotCargo/Weld/`. Numbers: the `Weld` tunables section. Requirements 2480 to 2499.

2480. **Tool.** The build window of a human player's own ship has a Weld button next to the Cut button. It works like the other build tools.
2481. **Targets.** A target is a ship within `Range` (40) of the ship's hull that is one of these: another ship that the player commands (an own ship), or a derelict (the game's junk: no owner and no crew; wrecks, broken pieces, a defeated ship without crew, an abandoned station). A ship or station that someone else owns and mans is not a target; the rule is the same for ships and stations. A target must hold only parts of the ship's own part set (so no asteroids), and no contract, the relic or an abandoned-ship event may name it.
2482. **Ghost.** With the Weld tool, the nearest target shows as a ghost in the ship's grid: each of its parts as an outline at a tile position and a quarter turn. The ghost starts at the target's current place, rounded to the nearest tile and quarter turn, and then follows the cursor one tile at a time. R turns the ghost a quarter turn. Tab picks the next target. The outlines are green when the place is valid and red when it is not, and the cursor names the reason.
2483. **Valid place.** No ghost tile may cover a tile of the ship's parts or of its design. At least one ghost part must touch a ship part along a side, with both part rules allowing contact there (the contact rule of spec 2462). A left click on a valid place sends the order; on a place that is not valid, it shows the reason and plays an error sound.
2484. **Order.** The order goes through an MP input; the sim checks it again with the same rules and refuses it silently when it no longer passes. A ship has at most one weld order; a new one replaces the old one. A right click with the Weld tool cancels the ship's order.
2485. **Pull.** The ship pulls the target into its place: the target moves at up to `PullSpeed` (4) and turns at up to `TurnSpeed` (45 degrees per second) relative to the ship. At its place it snaps to the exact tile position and then holds there, moving with the ship, until the weld ends. A target that does not reach its place within `PullSeconds` (30) ends the order with a chat line ("The weld could not pull the piece into place").
2486. **Join.** When the target holds at its place, each edge where a ship part and a target part are in contact is one crew job, as a cut edge (spec 2464): deconstruction priority, one crew member, `EdgeSeconds` (3) of work, standing on the ship's side, construction beams, and work kept when the crew leave. Weld work is free.
2487. **Merge.** When every joint edge is done, the target becomes part of the ship. Each target part becomes a ship part at its place, built at once, with its health, and the design gains it. The target's stored resources move into the ship's storage by type (the game's storage rules); what does not fit drops next to the ship as loose cargo. Tech moves the same way. Doors follow the game's automatic door rule. The target's crew become the ship's crew, and the crew aboard the target keep their places. Then the target ship leaves the game. Its ship name, roles and ship AI settings go away.
2488. **Not a loss.** The target leaves the game without the game's lost-ship handling: a weld is not a destruction. A live escort or a protected ship is never a target (spec 2481), so no mission fails by a weld.
2489. **Changes during the weld.** When the target or the ship leaves the system, or when the place is no longer valid (a part built in the way), the order ends. A joint edge whose contact ends needs no more work.
2490. **Visible.** The ghost (spec 2482). During the join, every machine draws the joint edges on the ship in the art of the cut line (spec 2468): a torch glow with a stitch, a spark where crew work, and a dark seam when done.
2491. **Co-op and save.** Orders and cancels go through MP inputs. The pull, the work and the merge run in the sim tick and read and write the run state (`RunState.Weld`). A save and a load keep the orders, the pull and the work; the tick creates the jobs again.
2492. **Scope.** Only a human player's own ship, in its build window. The AI never welds.
2493. **Tunables.** `Enabled` (true), `Range` (40), `PullSpeed` (4), `TurnSpeed` (45), `PullSeconds` (30), `EdgeSeconds` (3).
2494. **Tests.** `weld-own` (a ship cut into two own ships is welded back: pull, join work, merge; health, resources and crew are kept), `weld-wreck` (a derelict piece is welded on; a place that covers a ship tile is refused) and the co-op step `weld.join` in `mp:coop-core`. Unit tests: `WeldModelTests`.
