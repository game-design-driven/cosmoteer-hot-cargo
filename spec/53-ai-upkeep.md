## 53. AI upkeep

How enemy AI ships keep fighting over a long fight: how long their calls for help take, and how they get repairs. These rules apply to every hostile AI of requirement 360. Tunables: `Tactics`. Code: `Ai/CallStrategy.cs`, `Ai/FleeStrategy.cs`, `Tactics/FallBackStrategy.cs`, `Tactics/Resupply.cs`, `Tactics/ResourcePool.cs`, `Tactics/ShareResourcesHud.cs`. Pure math: `Tactics/RepairModel.cs`, `Tactics/PoolModel.cs`.

### Call interception

1300. **Call time.** A call for help (requirements 196 to 199) takes `CallSeconds` (default 22 s). When the call starts, the game checks every human ship that is an enemy of the caller. If one of them has an operational part of a type in `CallInterceptParts` (default the vanilla sensor array `cosmoteer.sensor_array`) whose radar range reaches the caller, the call takes `CallInterceptSeconds` (default 30 s) instead. The call starts at the same time in both cases. It only takes longer. An unknown part ID in `CallInterceptParts` is an error that shows in the overlay.
1301. **Sensor reach.** The radar range of a part is the vanilla radar radius of one sensor (`SensorManager.RadarRadius`): the ship's bounding radius + the part's `RadarRadius` × the radar factor of the ship's nebulas. The distance is from ship center to ship center. Limit: the check runs only at the call start, so a sensor ship that comes into range later does not slow a running call. Revisit when players ask to intercept a running call.
1302. **Tell.** An intercepted call shows a log line to every player, with the part name and the caller: "Sensor array: distress call from {ship} intercepted early. It takes longer." The ring and the "CALLING HELP n%" label of requirement 197 use the duration of that call.
1303. **Determinism.** The check reads sim state only (ship positions, operational sensors, nebulas) in the AI tick, and walks the human ships in sim order. The call duration is module state: saved with the ship and part of the integrity hash. The autotest `ai-call` checks both durations. The co-op step `ai.personalities` intercepts calls on two machines with no desync.

### Retreat and repair

These rules extend fall back (requirement 47) for every AI that runs it (requirement 360). Vanilla facts they build on: an AI ship repairs only with crew and the construction resources on board or on ships of its own player within 400 (`CareerSimModeManager.GetConstructionMode` gives `CrewResources`; `ResourceManager.GetUsableConstructionResourceTotal`). The combat AIs Normal, Easy, Wanderer, StationGuard and Police have no repair module (`Data/ai`), so fall back and flee are their only repair. Crews walk to other ships and to loose resources only through airlocks, and many combat ships have no storage bays. Next to a trading station the vanilla repair module queues nothing for an AI ship: the final cost turns the resources that the station sells into credits (`ShipUpdateInfo.GetTotalPhysicalCost`), and an AI player has none (`AISelfRepairModule.TryRepairObject`). The no-build block near combat (`EnemyCombatNoBuildRadius`, `EnemyCombatNoBuildDuration`) applies only to the instant build modes (`CareerSimModeManager.GetConstructionLegality`), so neither AI ships nor players in the normal crew build mode have it. It also only gates new player build orders: crews finish repairs that are already queued.

1305. **Repair plan.** When a ship starts to fall back, it measures what it lacks: the vanilla cost of all its repairs (every damaged part and every destroyed part and door, `ShipUpdateInfo.GetTotalPhysicalCost`) minus its usable construction resources, per resource type, and its free crew places (`ShipCrewManager.RemainingCrewCapacity`). It is short of crew when its crew is below `FallBackCrewFraction` (default 0.5) of its crew places. Then it picks the first plan that applies:
     1. **Cover**: it lacks no resource, is not short of crew, and a rock gives cover from the nearest enemy (requirement 1306).
     2. **Pool**: it lacks a resource, and a faction mate holds some of it (requirement 1307).
     3. **Wreck**: it lacks a resource, and the scavenge pick finds a wreck or loose resources for it (requirement 1321).
     4. **Station**: it lacks a resource or is short of crew, and a friendly station is in the system (requirement 1308).
     5. **Away**: the fall back of requirement 47: it flies `FallBackDistance` away from the nearest enemy and repairs with what it has.
1306. **Cover.** The ship flies to the hiding spot behind a rock of requirement 366 (the same rocks, search range and gap), on the far side from the nearest enemy human ship on its team radar. The spot follows the threat. When the ship is within `FallBackArriveDistance` (default 100) of the spot and the rock blocks the line to the threat, it holds there and repairs. A lost or unusable rock makes it pick the plan again. With no enemy on radar, it repairs where it is.
1307. **Faction pool.** AI ships of one faction (the same team: a faction's military and civilian ships, or one pirate or hunter group; never a special negative player index) share construction resources and help each other build like the ships of one player, when `FactionPoolEnabled` (default on) is on: the vanilla pool within 400 (`ResourceManager.GetUsableConstructionResourceShips`), crews that carry resources, and construction help in the Owned mode (`SimModeManager.CanShipHelpWithConstruction`) all use `Ship.IsSamePlayerAs`, which counts such two ships as the same player. Stations, junk and player ships keep the vanilla answer. Vanilla fetches stock from another ship with a transfer job that is valid only while the source ship is commandable by the job ship's player (`ResourceTransferJob.IsSourceValid`); during that one check, a source ship that pools with the job's ship counts as commandable, and nothing else changes. The pool plan: the ship picks the nearest faction mate within `FallBackPoolRange` (default 3,000, edge to edge) that holds at least 1 unit of a resource that the ship lacks (ties: lower ID), flies to `FallBackDockGap` (default 150) from its hull, then holds there and repairs from the pool, within the trip time (requirement 1309). No units move until its crews fetch them. A combat ship without storage bays repairs this way too. A faction mate that dies or stops being of the faction makes the ship pick the plan again.
1308. **Station.** A friendly station is a station that trades with the ship (`ShipAI.WillTradeWith`, the station list of requirement 1069; stations in stasis count when they are not enemies of the ship). Pirates have none. The ship flies to the nearest one (ties: lower ID) to `FallBackDockGap` from its hull. When the station allows trade with it (`SimModeManager.AllowResourceTrade`, within 400), the station supplies it once per visit, for free (enemy AIs have no money limit): each resource that the ship's own storage lacks for its repairs, as far as its storage has room, and crew for the free crew places (`ShipCrewManager.CreateCrewsAtRandomParts`). Then the ship holds still next to the station, and the station repairs `FallBackStationRepairsPerTick` (default 2) of its damaged or destroyed parts and doors at once in each AI check, for free (`ShipUpdateInfo.ApplyPhysical`). A station that is gone or becomes an enemy makes the ship pick the plan again.
1309. **Time and return.** The ship returns at `FallBackRecoverFraction`, as in requirement 47. The time limit is `FallBackMaxSeconds` for cover and away, and `FallBackTripSeconds` (default 180 s) for pool, wreck and station, counted from the fall back start. The cooldown after a return is the time limit of the last plan.
1310. **Tells.** Movement only (requirement 368): the ship breaks off, flies behind a rock, next to an ally or to a station, sits still while its crew repair, and comes back repaired. No label, marker or log line shows the plan. `Debug.TacticsLog` names each plan, transfer and supply. The station repair has no crew walk: the parts mend while the ship sits at the station.
1311. **Determinism and switch.** Plans, faction mates, stations and rocks are picked in the AI tick from sim state, by distance, ties by ID. The plan, its target ID, its place and the supplied flag are module state: saved with the ship and part of the integrity hash. Crew spawn uses the ship's sim random source. `FallBackResupplyEnabled` (default on) switches requirements 1305 to 1309 off: the ship then always uses the away plan.
1312. **Scavenge.** Repairs from shipwrecks and loose resources are the wreck plan of requirement 1321, between pool and station in `RepairModel.PickPlan`.
1313. **Tests.** Unit tests: `RepairModelTests` (plan order, lacking amounts) and `PoolModelTests` (the pool rule). Autotests: `tactics-fallback-cover`, `tactics-fallback-pool` (a Monolith combat ship without storage repairs from a Monolith trade ship of another player index), `tactics-fallback-station` (a damaged faction ship without resources and short of crew flies to a station, gets crew and repairs, and returns). The co-op step `ai.personalities` runs fall back with supply on two machines with no desync.

### Flee to a station

1314. **Flee to a station.** A fleeing ship that is not a corporate hunter (requirement 195) first keeps `FleeDistance` from the nearest enemy, as before. When it is clear, it checks once per flee what it lacks (requirement 1305). If it lacks a resource or is short of crew and a friendly station is in the system (requirement 1308), it flies to the nearest one, ties to the lower ID. An enemy within `FleeDistance` on the way makes it keep away first, then go on. Else it repairs where it is, as before.
1315. **At the station.** The station supplies the ship once per visit and repairs it, exactly as in requirement 1308 (`FallBackStationRepairsPerTick`). The ship holds still there until the flee ends (`FleeRecoverFraction` with weapons, requirement 195). An unarmed ship stays at the station. A station that is gone or becomes an enemy makes the ship repair where it is. Tells: movement only (requirement 368).
1316. **Determinism and tests.** The check, the station ID and the supplied flag are flee module state: saved with the ship and part of the integrity hash. They reset when a new flee starts. `FallBackResupplyEnabled` off switches requirements 1314 and 1315 off. The autotest `ai-flee-station` checks that a damaged fleeing faction ship short of crew docks at a station, gets crew and fights again.

### Repairs hold in combat

1317. **Combat hold.** An AI ship that is hostile to the players is in combat while an enemy ship within the combat difficulty's `EnemyCombatNoBuildRadius` (800 on easy to very hard, 0 on no death) fired within its `EnemyCombatNoBuildDuration` (5 s): the players' block rule of the instant build modes. In combat, the ship cancels its queued repairs and rebuilds (`ConstructionManager.CancelAll`; destroyed parts keep their rebuild marks), and fall back and flee queue none. Exceptions, as designed in requirements 1306, 1307, 1308 and 1315: a ship that holds in cover behind its rock, a ship next to its faction mate in the pool plan, and a ship next to its station in a fall back or flee. The check runs in each AI check of the personality module, which runs first in every hostile AI. `RepairCombatHoldEnabled` (default on) switches it off. Limit: ships that are not hostile to the players (AI against AI fights) keep the vanilla behavior; revisit if such fights matter. Measured before this rule: a ship that returned from a fall back with 61 repairs queued finished 24 of them in 20 s under fire. The autotest `tactics-combat-repair` checks that such a ship cancels them.

### Shared resources in co-op

1318. **Share resources switch.** In co-op (two or more human players), a "Share resources" switch sits right of the ship tool buttons, on their row (in the tool button column, above the vanilla panels there, while an open window covers that spot or no ship is selected), above every vanilla panel in its column (the game's own list of UI that covers the view, `GameGui.IndicatorBlockers`: the ships card, the tool buttons, the open toolbox), under the listen-in list (requirement 1132). It looks like the buttons of that row: a vanilla ship button (`ShipGui.SHIP_UI_BUTTON`, 50 x 50, as the cancel button of the row), 10 px from them, with an icon that is green while sharing is on and HUD blue while it is off, as the vanilla on/off tool icons. Its tooltip names the state ("Share resources: ON" or "OFF") and what a press does. Any human player can press it. A press sends the MP input `HotCargoShareResources`; the sim flips the switch and every player gets a chat line that names who turned sharing on or off. While it is on, the ships of all human players count as the same player for construction (the rule of requirement 1307): they share construction and repair resources within the vanilla pool range (400), crews carry and fetch across them, and they help each other build, like the ships of one player's fleet. Off (the default): vanilla behavior. A player never pools with an AI ship.
1319. **One rule, state and hash.** `PoolModel.Pools` decides for every pair: the same player index (vanilla), two AI ships of one faction (requirement 1307), or two human ships while the switch is on. The switch is run state (`RunState.SharedResources`): saved with the run and part of the co-op integrity hash. All decisions read sim and run state only.
1320. **Tests.** Unit test `PoolModelTests`. The co-op step `tactics.share-resources` (`tests/autotest-mp/coop-economy.txt`): player 1's ship has no steel and sits next to player 0's ship; the client turns sharing on with the real input; both machines show the switch on; the client can see the switch (`share_hud`: on the screen and under no vanilla panel or open window) with no ship selected, with a ship selected and with the comms box open; player 1's usable steel grows by player 0's stock; and player 1's crew rebuild a part with player 0's steel.

### Repairs from wrecks and loose resources

1321. **Wreck plan.** The ship picks its target with the scavenge pick of requirement 1333 (`Scavenger.FindTarget`): the nearest ripe wreck on its team radar or ripe loose resource in its sight, within `Scavenge.SearchRange`, never a mission item, a tech crate, the relic or a wreck that someone else salvages. The pick needs free room in the ship's storage bays, so a ship without storage bays never takes this plan. Each AI check, the ship works on the current nearest target as a scavenger does (`Scavenger.Work`: salvage beside a wreck, or crews fetch loose resources; requirements 1334, 1335). When it lacks no resource any more, or no target is left (none in range, or its storage is full), it picks the plan again (requirement 1305): with the resources now on board, that is usually cover or away, where it repairs. It does not sell. The time limit is `FallBackTripSeconds` (requirement 1309).
1322. **Determinism and tests.** The plan reads sim and run state only (the scavenge age check of requirement 1332 is run state) and runs in the AI tick, so co-op machines agree. `FallBackResupplyEnabled` off switches it off with the other plans; with scavenging off (`Scavenge.Enabled`), no target is ripe and the plan never applies. Unit test: `RepairModelTests` (the plan order with the wreck). Autotest `tactics-fallback-wreck`: a damaged pirate cargo ship with no faction mate and no friendly station salvages a ripe wreck, then repairs. Loose resources count only in the ship's sight, which hardly reaches past the vanilla reach of 400, so in play the plan is mostly about wrecks on radar. The salvage and collect steps themselves are the scavenge functions, tested by `scavenge-basic`.

### Steady AI states and sane firepower

Telemetry of a co-op playtest: neutral traders switched between trading and fleeing 40 to 60 times in 10 to 25 minutes (one 119 times in 24 minutes, every 7 to 9 s), and hostile ships with the Cover trait switched between cover and patrol every 6 to 8 s for 5 minutes. A captured ship with one missile launcher reported a vanilla firepower of 50,000, 10 times the mothership's. Code: `Ai/SteadyStates.cs`, `Ai/Firepower.cs`, `Tactics/CoverStrategy.cs`. Pure math: `Ai/AiModel.cs`.

1531. **Trader flee hysteresis.** The vanilla flee module of trade ships (`AIFleeFromEnemiesModule`, `ai_trade_ship.rules`) starts and stops at the same range: an enemy inside the ship's radar radius (its `FleeStartRange` and `FleeStopRange` are unset, so the radar radius caps both). A player at the edge of that radius made the trader flee 1,000, lose the contact, turn back to trade and flee again. Now a fleeing trade ship keeps fleeing while an enemy is within its radar radius × `TraderFleeStopFactor` (default 1.75): it holds its last flee move and does not turn back. It starts to flee as in vanilla. The flee flag is the vanilla module's own saved state, so co-op machines agree.
1532. **Cover only in a fight.** A ship starts a hide phase (requirement 366) only while it attacks the cover target (its confirmed attack command is on that target). A ship whose vanilla AI does not attack the target (for example a Wanderer outside its patrol area) no longer hides from it and patrols in turn. The fight and hide rhythm itself (`CoverPeekSeconds`, `CoverHideSeconds`) stays: that alternation between the cover module and the vanilla attack module is the tell of requirement 366, not a flip.
1533. **Firepower.** Where Hot Cargo weighs firepower (ship strength of requirement 1520, so the risk model and the friendly help checks; regroup, requirement 201; the scavenger's shyness; convoy target value), it uses the vanilla firepower rating of each weapon that vanilla would estimate (`Weapon.ShouldEstimateFirepower`: not point defense, and with its attack toggle on where it has one), each capped at `FirepowerWeaponCap` (default 8,000, above the highest fixed vanilla rating, the resonance beam's 7,500). The vanilla total (`WeaponManager.GetTotalFirePower`) counts weapons in defend mode and has no cap: a missile launcher rates at the damage per second of its current missile (50,000 for one launcher). Telemetry keeps reporting the vanilla total. A flak platform in defend mode has no firepower against ships, which is right: it does not shoot ships.
1534. **No flip in the risk model.** The risk stance (requirement 1524) changes only how one-time events go: the demand hail and the surrender offer fire at most once per ship (vanilla clears the ship's will after a hail), and the flee decision is one roll per drop below the flee health (requirement 193). So a confidence near a threshold cannot make a ship switch back and forth.
1535. **Tests.** Unit test: `AiModelTests` (the capped firepower sum). Autotest `ai-trader-flee`: a trade ship with an enemy just outside its radar radius after it fled keeps fleeing and does not turn back to trade.
