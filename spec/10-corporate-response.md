## 10. Corporate response

30. `HeatStages` is a list of stages. Each stage has a threshold and a response. The list decides the stage count, and the last stage is the Strike stage. Warning is above the highest heat that a jump carries in (requirement 650), so it always starts after arrival. Hunt starts with the station markup (requirement 655). Defaults:

| Stage | Heat | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Warning | 40 | Alert sound and message. Scouts: 1 ship of the sector tier in sector 1, 2 in the last sector (requirement 190). A scout can fly to a relay to report (requirement 910). |
| Hunt | 75 | Three hunter ships of the sector tier + 1. They can park at a relay (blockade, requirement 915). |
| Strike | 100 | A strike force: 2 ships of the sector tier + 1 in sector 1, up to 3 ships of tier + 2 in the last sector (requirement 190). The ships arrive one at a time, 45 s apart (requirement 191). A new wave every `StrikeWaveIntervalSeconds` (default 180 s) × the Pursuit interval factor (requirement 901) while heat stays at 100. |

31. Each stage starts once for each sector visit. The ship counts of the table are for the last sector of the run; the Pursuit sends fewer early and moves them with the fleet value (requirements 901 to 903). The Strike stage repeats its waves. A stage response can arrive in groups (requirement 191). A group spawns only enough ships to reach `MaxLiveHunters` (default 12) live hunters in the system. In co-op the group and the cap scale (requirement 334).
32. Hunters spawn at a distance of `SpawnDistanceMin` to `SpawnDistanceMax` (default 2,000 to 3,000) from one player ship (requirement 2242), and at least `SpawnDistanceMin` from every station (live or in stasis), so no hunter arrives in a station's guns. All ships of one wave come from one direction, spread over `WaveArcDegrees` (default 40°). They always know where the players are, move to them and attack. Hunters far from the players move toward them in stasis too.
2241. **Spawn anchor.** A spawn for the players (hunter waves, ships that event outcomes spawn, event carriers, the Wayfarers, and the spawns of requirement 2240) goes near one player ship, the anchor, never near the center of the player fleet. In co-op the players can be far apart: the center can then be far from every player ship, and vanilla puts ships that spawn or stay out of the live range (about 2,500 from a player ship) into stasis, so they never reach the players. The anchor is the player ship with the highest weight of that spawn; ties go to the lowest player index, then the lowest ship ID (`SimQueries.AnchorShip`). Event outcomes weigh the ships of the player who chose; carriers have no weight (the first player ship); the Wayfarers weigh the enemy strength near each ship (requirement 1370).
2242. **Hunter anchor.** Hunters spawn near the player ship that carries the most stolen tech. Carried tech sets the heat multiplier (requirement 24), so the corporation sends its hunters after the ship that holds it. Without tech on any ship, the tie rule of requirement 2241 picks the anchor.
2243. **Test.** Co-op session `coop-combat`, step `hunters.anchor`: the client flies 16,000 away from the host, a hunter wave spawns, and after the stasis update the hunters are live, with none in stasis. With the old fleet-center spawn the step fails (the hunters go into stasis).
2240. **More anchors.** These spawns use the anchor of requirement 2241 too, each with its own weight. Debt collectors (requirement 1407): a ship of the player who owes the debt (requirement 1401). Debt helpers (requirement 1431): the ship with the most enemy ships within `HelpFightRadius`, the one in the fight. The hacker (requirement 930): the ship that the pursuit hunts, the same ship as the hunter anchor (requirement 2242), because he sells an outage against that pursuit. Event drops (`DropNear`, requirement 1141): a ship of the player who chose, who gets the goods. The relic ship (requirement 765) and chain Arrival missions: no weight (the first player ship), because they belong to no player. The guard step of requirement 2243 covers the shared rule; these spawns need no other check.
33. Hunters are a corporate force, not a rules faction. They use built-in ships with the `ShipTags` tag (default `combat`) from the `ShipFactions` pools (default: all factions), at the wanted tier or the nearest lower tier. Their names start with `ShipNamePrefix` (default "Corporate"). All hunters in a system share one hostile player index, so they are allies of each other and enemies of the players. A new wave takes the index of the hunters in the system, live or in stasis, and only an allocated index (MaxPlayers and above). A hunter ship that the players took over is no longer a hunter, and new hunters never take a human index. Like vanilla ambushes, they are also enemies of faction ships, except the ally faction's ships in corporate ally space (requirement 1150). Every hunter carries FTL fuel (requirement 192). Hunters give no fame and no money reward, so a fight with them adds no heat; their wreck pays in salvage, about 10 % of the ship value on the Fast economy (BALANCE.md). Hunters never offer or accept surrender through the vanilla dialogue; the diplomacy hails of section 37 handle their demands and surrenders. Resolved: a rules faction needs an icon, two player indices and its own ship pool, and the faction reputation systems would treat it as a normal faction. A unique player index gives the same result at much lower cost.
34. Hunters prefer to attack ships that carry Tech Vaults. A ship with a Tech Vault part counts `VaultTargetFactor` times more (default 3) when a hunter picks the nearest target.
35. The Strike stage is dangerous but survivable. Its ships arrive in groups, so players who can fight have time to destroy a hunter, take its fuel and jump away (requirements 191 and 192). It does not end the run directly. Only a loss under requirement 9 ends the run. **Open decision:** the user may choose a hard fail instead.
