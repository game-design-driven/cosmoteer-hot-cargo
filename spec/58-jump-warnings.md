## 58. Jump warnings and the stranded fleet

The players see which ships stay behind on a system jump, and why, before they jump. When no ship is left in the current system, they see where the fleet waits and how to go there. Code: `src/HotCargo/Ux/JumpWarnings.cs`, the pure rules in `Ux/JumpWarningsModel.cs`. Switch: `Ux.JumpWarnings`. Playtest 2026-11-15: after a fight the players took ships and jumped. Most taken ships had no hyperdrive, and the mothership was too big for its one drive, so they stayed behind. The one ship that jumped died, and the run was stuck with no ship in the system.

1560. **Why a ship stays.** Vanilla jumps only the human ships with `FtlManager.IsReadyToJump` (`GameRoot.DoHumanPlayersFtlJump`). A ship that is not ready stays for the first of these reasons: no working hyperdrive; no control (`CommandManager.CanExecuteCommands`: command points used above command points provided); too big (the jump costs more hyperium than its working drives can hold); not enough hyperium; not ordered to jump; drives still spinning up. The cost is the vanilla `FtlManager.GetTotalFtlMapJumpFuelCost` along the vanilla jump path, so the blind jump factor (requirement 651) counts. It grows with mass, one drive gives at most 50 % efficiency (`JumpEfficiency`), and tiles far from every drive give less (`JumpEfficiencyDistanceRange`). Drives hold 20, 40 or 80 hyperium (`hyperdrive_small`, `_med`, `_large`). A too big ship shows NEED FUEL in vanilla, but no amount of fuel fits.
1561. **Before a jump is planned.** A ship has a lasting reason to stay when it has no working hyperdrive, has no control, or is too big for the cheapest jump from the current system (the cheapest vanilla path to a connected system). A ship without a hyperdrive counts only while a human ship of the fleet has one: the starter ships have none, and before the first drive no ship can jump, so the warning is noise. Fuel and spin-up do not count here: vanilla shows them, and the player fixes them on the way.
1562. **Hover line.** The hover text of an own or shared ship (the local player index, requirement 1139) with a lasting reason gets a red line "Stays behind on a jump: <reason>" and a second line with the numbers and the fix: build a hyperdrive; crew a control room; add a hyperdrive or make the ship smaller.
1563. **Join notice.** When an own or shared ship with a lasting reason joins the fleet (a capture, a new ship), one chat line and one banner name the ship, the reason and the fix. Each ship gets it once per system visit. A load or a jump arrival only takes the baseline, so ships that were already there give no notice.
1564. **Jump window.** In the vanilla spin-up window, a ship that is too big shows TOO BIG in place of NEED FUEL, and a ship with drives but no control shows NO CONTROL in place of NO DRIVE. Each ship that stays has a tooltip with its reason, the numbers and the fix.
1565. **Jump dialog.** The vanilla dialog on ENGAGE (when a local ship is not ready) lists each local ship that stays, with its reason, then asks "Jump anyway?". It is the only pop-up: no new dialog. The rest is the vanilla flow: no dialog when every local ship is ready, and the jump goes ahead unless the player picks the second option.
1566. **Stranded fleet.** The players are stranded when no human ship is in the current system, no jump is under way (a local jump, the node jump, or ships in jump limbo), and human ships wait in another system (the vanilla count per system, `CareerGameModeManager._nodeData`, set when the game leaves a system). The way back is the vanilla switch: a click on that system in the galaxy map (`MPJumpToNodeInput`, no fuel, no heat cut, requirement 650; in co-op all players vote). The loss timer stays paused (requirement 12), because the waiting ships can hold tech: a timer that runs anyway would end a run that still has its tech in another system. The target is the system with the most waiting ships; a tie goes to the first by system name.
1567. **Stranded text.** While the players are stranded, the heat panel shows a red line: "No ship here. Your fleet waits in <system>. Click Go to <system>, or open the map (<map key>) and click <system>.", and under it the recall countdown (requirement 1601). When it lasts 6 seconds (after the "SHIP LOST" banner of the last ship), one short center text ("NO SHIP HERE. YOUR FLEET WAITS IN <system>") and one chat line with the full text show once. A new system visit starts quiet.
1568. **UI only.** Requirements 1560 to 1567 and 1602 read sim and map state and change none: no sim state, no MP input of their own, no save. The recall (requirement 1601) sends only the vanilla system switch input. Each machine shows the warnings of its own ships. `Ux.JumpWarnings` off gives the vanilla window, dialog and hover text, and no notices, stranded line or recall.
1569. **Tests.** Unit tests `JumpWarningsModelTests`: the reason order, too big against fuel, no drive only with a fleet drive, the cheapest jump, the join watch, the stranded target. Autotest `ux-jump-warnings`: a copy of the starter ship stands in for a taken ship; no warning before the first drive; the hover line and one join notice after it; too big with a raised blind fuel factor; the real ENGAGE click lists both ships that stay, "no" keeps the fleet, "yes" jumps.
1601. **Recall.** A run never stays stranded (requirement 1566). Vanilla always allows the switch to a system with human ships (`CareerGameModeManager.AllowMPInput`, `MPJumpToNodeInput`), so the waiting fleet is always reachable, and the loss timer stays paused (requirement 12): the waiting ships can hold the tech. While the players are stranded, a "Go to <system>" button shows under the area name text, and the heat panel counts down `Ux.RecallSeconds` (default 30 s, real time). Then the game sends the vanilla switch to that system by itself: in single player at once, in co-op from the host, so the vanilla unanimous vote runs. A failed vote starts the countdown again. 0 turns the automatic recall off; the button stays. The switch costs no fuel and cuts no heat (requirement 650). Playtest 2026-11-15: the captured hunter Gonolek jumped alone to Titraness, the mothership with the last tech unit stayed in Vintecco with 3 more ships, Gonolek died, and the run stayed running with no ship in the system. Autotest `ux-stranded-recall` rebuilds that case: a ship without tech jumps alone, dies, the recall goes out, and the players are back with the carrier and its tech. Unit tests: the recall delay and its repeat after a failed vote.
1602. **Tech stays behind.** In the jump window tooltip (requirement 1564) and the jump dialog (requirement 1565), a ship that stays and holds tech gets "(carries <n> tech)". When no ship that jumps holds tech and a ship that stays holds some, the dialog starts with "All your tech stays behind." Unit tests: the all-tech rule.
