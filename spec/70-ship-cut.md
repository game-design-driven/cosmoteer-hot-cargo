## 70. Cut ships apart

In vanilla, a ship splits only when its parts lose contact, for example when an explosion destroys the parts between two halves (`StructureContiguityManager.SplitIntoNewShips`). Two parts are in contact when they touch along a side and both part rules allow contact on that side (`PartRules.AllowedContiguity`; no vanilla part allows contact at a corner). With the cut, the player draws a line in the ship editor, and the crew cut the ship along it. Code: `src/HotCargo/Cut/`. Numbers: the `Cut` tunables section. Requirements 2460 to 2479.

2460. **Tool.** The build window of a human player's own ship has a Cut button next to the remove and select buttons. It works like the other build tools: select it, then use it on the ship.
2461. **Line.** The player presses the left mouse button on a corner between tiles and drags. The line follows the edges between tiles, from corner to corner. Dragging back over the last edge removes it. An edge between two tiles of one part (inside a 2x1 part, for example) is blocked: the line does not go through it and the blocked edge shows red, so the player routes around the part. Edges outside the ship are allowed, so the line can start and end in space.
2462. **Check.** A line edge needs work when the tiles on its two sides hold two different parts that are in contact across it. When the player releases the button, the line must cut the ship into exactly two pieces, and the two sides of every edge that needs work must be in different pieces. Otherwise a short message at the cursor names the reason, an error sound plays, and nothing changes: no edge needs work ("Cut line must cross the ship"), the ship stays one piece ("Cut must go all the way across"), more than two pieces ("Cut makes more than two pieces"), or an edge joins tiles of one piece ("Cut line has a loose end").
2463. **Order.** A line that passes the check becomes a cut order through an MP input. The sim checks the line again with the same rule and refuses it silently when it no longer passes. A ship has at most one order. A new order on the same ship replaces the old one and keeps the work done on edges that are in both. A right click with the Cut tool on a ship with an order cancels the order.
2464. **Work.** Each edge of an order that needs work is one crew job. The job uses the priority of deconstruction work (`Ship.Rules.DefaultDeconstructPriority`, as `DeconstructDoorJob`). One crew member works an edge. The crew member stands next to the edge, in the tile of the piece that stays (spec 2465). An edge takes `EdgeSeconds` (3) seconds of crew work (crew `WorkPerSecond`). The crew member shows construction beams while it works. When the crew member leaves the job, the edge keeps its work, and the job goes back to the job queue. Cut work is free.
2465. **Separation.** The pieces stay one ship until the work on every edge is done. Then the game's own split runs with the cut edges as gaps. The piece that stays the ship is the game's choice: the piece with the strongest control room (`ContiguityOriginPriority`), then the larger piece. The other piece becomes a new ship, as in a vanilla split: same owner, a design made from its parts, and the crew on it stay with it.
2466. **Design trim.** At the separation, the design of the ship that stays loses every design part on the far side of the cut: each design part that does not connect to the parts of the ship that stays without crossing a cut edge. So the crew do not rebuild the cut-off piece. Destroyed and unbuilt design parts on the far side go too.
2467. **Changes during the work.** An edge stops needing work when a part on one of its sides goes or the contact across it ends. The order then counts that edge as done. When the last edge is done but the ship does not split (for example, a new part bridges the line), the order ends, the ship stays one ship, and a chat line says "The cut did not split the ship". An order whose ship is not in the current system (destroyed, or left behind at a jump) ends.
2468. **Visible.** Every machine draws the lines of every order on its ship in the world: edges that need work in the color of the remove tool, done edges dim. While the player drags, the Cut tool draws the line in progress and the blocked edge.
2469. **Co-op and save.** Orders and cancels go through MP inputs. The work and the separation run in the sim tick and read and write the run state (`RunState.Cut`: the orders with their edges and work). So every co-op machine splits the same ship at the same tick. A save and a load keep the orders and the work. The jobs are not in the save: the tick creates them again, and crew take them again.
2470. **Scope.** Only a human player's own ship, in its build window. The AI never cuts. A ship in a jump or with construction not allowed (`Ship.IsConstructionLegal`) gets no new order.
2471. **Tunables.** `Enabled` (true) turns the Cut button and the work on or off. `EdgeSeconds` (3) is the crew work per edge.
2472. **Tests.** `cut-split` (a clean cut queues work, the ship splits only after the last edge, the design loses the far side, and the order survives a save and a load), `cut-refused` (a line through a 2x1 part, a line that does not go across and a loose end are refused), `cut-resume` (work stops when crew leave, the edges keep their work, and the cut ends after the crew come back) and the co-op step `cut.split` in `mp:coop-core`. Unit tests: `CutModelTests`.
