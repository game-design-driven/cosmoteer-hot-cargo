## 70. Cut ships apart

In vanilla, a ship splits only when its parts lose contact, for example when an explosion destroys the parts between two halves (`StructureContiguityManager.SplitIntoNewShips`). Two parts are in contact when they touch along a side and both part rules allow contact on that side (`PartRules.AllowedContiguity`; no vanilla part allows contact at a corner). With the cut, the player draws a line in the ship editor, and the crew cut the ship along it. Code: `src/HotCargo/Cut/`. Numbers: the `Cut` tunables section. Requirements 2460 to 2479.

2460. **Tool.** The build window of a human player's own ship has a Cut button next to the remove and select buttons. It works like the other build tools: select it, then use it on the ship.
2461. **Line.** The player presses the left mouse button on a corner between tiles and drags. The line follows the edges between tiles, from corner to corner. Dragging back over the last edge removes it. An edge between two tiles of one part (inside a 2x1 part, for example) is blocked: the line does not go through it, the blocked edge shows the remove tool's red X and the cursor says "Cannot cut through a part", so the player routes around the part. The line can turn at any corner and can close on itself. Edges outside the ship are allowed, so the line can start and end in space.
2462. **Check.** When the player releases the button, the line is a cut when its edges split the ship's parts into two or more connected pieces: a line across the ship, an L or a stair that cuts off a corner, a closed loop that cuts out an inner block, or lines that cross. Otherwise the message "Cut must cut off a part" shows at the end of the line, an error sound plays, and nothing changes. A line edge needs work when the tiles on its two sides hold two different parts that are in contact across it and lie in different pieces. Other edges of the line need no work: edges in space, between parts that are not in contact, or loose ends inside one piece.
2463. **Order.** A line that passes the check becomes a cut order through an MP input, and it follows the vanilla edit flow (spec 2475). The sim checks the line again with the same rule and refuses it silently when it no longer passes. A ship has at most one order. A new order on the same ship replaces the old one and keeps the work done on edges that are in both. A right click with the Cut tool on a ship with an order cancels the order. The work starts only when the order is applied (spec 2475).
2464. **Work.** Each edge of an order that needs work is one crew job. The job uses the priority of deconstruction work (`Ship.Rules.DefaultDeconstructPriority`, as `DeconstructDoorJob`). One crew member works an edge. The crew member works from the tile next to the edge on the side of the piece that stays (spec 2465): a walkable tile (a corridor, a room) they reach inside the ship; any other tile (armor) they reach from outside, as vanilla construction crew do (EVA through an airlock, `ConstructPartJob`). The crew search for such a tile starts at a point near the hull, as `RepairPartJob` does: the crew path grid covers only walkable tiles and a margin, and a search from a tile outside it crashes the game. An edge takes `EdgeSeconds` (3) seconds of crew work (crew `WorkPerSecond`). The crew member shows construction beams while it works. When the crew member leaves the job, the edge keeps its work, and the job goes back to the job queue. Cut work is free.
2465. **Separation.** The pieces stay one ship until the work on every edge is done. Then the game's own split runs with the cut edges as gaps, and a heavy clunk sounds (spec 2474). The piece that stays the ship is the game's choice: the piece with the strongest control room (`ContiguityOriginPriority`), then the larger piece. Each other piece becomes a new ship, as in a vanilla split: same owner, its own design made from its own parts, and the crew aboard it at the separation go with it. Each piece then keeps its owner or becomes a derelict by the vanilla rule (`CareerSimModeManager.GetAllegianceForReducedShip`): it stays the player's ship when crew are assigned to it and it has a working control room, crew aboard or crew quarters; else it becomes a derelict. A working Computer also keeps it the player's (spec 1059). So a cut through a ship with a control room in each half gives two own ships. Each new piece then drifts away from the ship at `SeparationSpeed` (2) relative to it, so the split shows; their momentum stays the same. A piece that a closed loop cut out fits its hole exactly, so the walls of the hole stop the drift: it stays in the hole as its own piece and rides along when the ship flies and turns. It comes free when the parts around it go.
2466. **Design trim.** At the separation, the design of the ship that stays loses every design part on the far side of the cut: each design part that does not connect to the parts of the ship that stays without crossing a cut edge. So the crew do not rebuild the cut-off piece. Destroyed and unbuilt design parts on the far side go too.
2467. **Changes during the work.** An edge stops needing work when a part on one of its sides goes or the contact across it ends. The order then counts that edge as done. When the last edge is done but the ship does not split (for example, a new part bridges the line), the order ends, the ship stays one ship, and a chat line says "The cut did not split the ship". An order whose ship is not in the current system (destroyed, or left behind at a jump) ends.
2468. **Visible.** Every machine draws the lines of every order on its ship in the world, in the game's own art (vanilla textures by reference, `mod/cut/lines.rules`): an edge that waits for work glows like the crew salvage beam, a cutting torch, with a stitch across its middle; where crew work, a spark flickers on the edge; a done edge fades from the glow to a dark seam in 0.8 seconds. An edge that needs no work shows nothing. While the player drags, the Cut tool draws the line in progress as marching amber dashes with stitches, and a blocked edge as the remove tool's red X.
2469. **Co-op and save.** Orders and cancels go through MP inputs. The work and the separation run in the sim tick and read and write the run state (`RunState.Cut`: the orders with their edges and work). So every co-op machine splits the same ship at the same tick. A save and a load keep the orders and the work. The jobs are not in the save: the tick creates them again, and crew take them again.
2470. **Scope.** Only a human player's own ship, in its build window. The AI never cuts. A ship in a jump or with construction not allowed (`Ship.IsConstructionLegal`) gets no new order.
2471. **Tunables.** `Enabled` (true) turns the Cut button and the work on or off. `EdgeSeconds` (3) is the crew work per edge. `SeparationSpeed` (2) is the speed at which the pieces drift apart.
2473. **Cursor.** While the Cut tool is active and no drag runs, the tile edge nearest to the cursor shows as a faint dashed edge with a corner mark at the corner nearest to the cursor, where a press starts the line.
2474. **Sounds.** All vanilla sounds by reference: selecting the tool plays the tool button sound of the build window. Each edge added while dragging plays the blueprint add sound (`BlueprintAddSound`); a blocked edge and a refused line play the error sound. A line that becomes a pending order plays the blueprint add sound; an applied order plays the vanilla "Make it so" sound; a canceled or discarded order plays the cancel sound (`CancelConstructionSound`). While crew work on an edge of a ship in view, the crew construction sound (`crew/construction.wav`) loops, at most one loop for all cuts. At the separation, the door deconstruct sound (`ships/common/sounds/door_deconstruct.wav`) plays as a clunk.
2475. **Apply like other edits.** A cut follows the vanilla edit flow of the build window. In blueprint mode a new cut order is pending: it shows as amber dashes with the other pending design edits, and no crew work on it. The vanilla "Make it so" button applies it with the other edits; the button can apply a pending cut alone. The vanilla "Discard changes" drops every pending cut of the ship with the other edits. In live mode a cut starts at once, as the remove tool's edits do, and the vanilla "Cancel all" button cancels it with the other queued construction. Apply, discard and cancel go through MP inputs. Weld orders (section 71) follow the same flow.
2476. **Removed.** (Command decides the owner: after a split, a new piece without command became a derelict, whatever crew or beds it had. User decision 2026-11-26: it blocked nothing that a cheap ship with a control room does not already give, so every piece follows the vanilla rule of spec 2465.)
2472. **Tests.** `cut-split` (a clean cut queues work, the ship splits only after the last edge, the design loses the far side, and the order survives a save and a load), `cut-refused` (a line that runs into a 2x1 part and a line that stops halfway are refused; a loose end does not spoil a cut), `cut-resume` (work stops when crew leave, the edges keep their work, and the cut ends after the crew come back), `cut-twin` (a ship with a control room in each half becomes two own ships, each with its own design and the crew aboard it) `cut-loop` (a closed loop cuts out an inner block that stays in its hole while the ship flies and turns), `cut-pending` (in blueprint mode a cut waits; Discard drops it; Make it so applies it alone; in live mode Cancel all cancels it) and the co-op step `cut.split` in `mp:coop-core`. Unit tests: `CutModelTests`.
