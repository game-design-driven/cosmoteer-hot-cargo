## 74. Run map and the lockdown line

The run map is a short, tapered map, like the sectors of FTL: a narrow start, a wide middle and one Buyer system at the end. The players move forward through it. Code: `src/HotCargo/Journey/RunMap.cs`, the pure rules in `Journey/RunMapModel.cs`. Rules: `mod/journey/galaxy.rules`. Numbers: the `Journey` tunables section. Requirements 2550 to 2579.

2550. **Shape.** The map has `Journey.RunSectorCount` (6) bands: band 0 holds the starting system, the last band holds the Buyer's system, and each band between holds 1 + (`RunSectorCount` − 2) × sin(π × band ÷ last band)^0.7 systems (rounded, at least 2). With 6 steps that is 1, 4, 5, 5, 4 and 1 systems, 20 in all. A band is 9 map units long (vanilla links are 5 to 10, `base_galaxy.rules`, so fuel costs stay about vanilla). Each system sits within 0.3 of a band from its band center, in rows 6 apart with a random shift of up to 0.15 of a row, so the map is longer than wide and has no straight columns. The band of a system follows from its X location, so the map saves nothing of its own. One tunable sets the size: the width and the tier ramp (requirement 2552) follow from the steps. Rules types `HotCargoRunMap` (systems and links, in place of the vanilla `MapNodes`) and `HotCargoRunMapTiers` (after the vanilla `Factions` spawner).
2551. **Links.** Each system links forward to the 2 or 3 nearest systems (by row) of the next band, 3 with chance 0.35. Systems of the last band before the Buyer link only to the Buyer. A system without a link from the band behind gets one from the nearest system there. Row neighbors in a band link sideways with chance 0.3. **Old lanes:** `RunSectorCount` ÷ 3 (2 with 6 steps) long links, each from a random system in band 1 to band last − 3, to the nearest system 2 or 3 bands ahead, never into the Buyer's band. An old lane saves jumps and lands several tiers higher; it costs the fuel of its length. So every system before the last band has at least 2 ways ahead, and the Buyer is reachable from every system.
2552. **Tiers.** The low tier of band b is 1 + round((`FinalSectorTier` − 1) × b ÷ last band); a system spans that tier to 2 tiers more. With 6 steps and final tier 12: 1, 3, 5, 8, 10 and 12. Every system has the tier of its band: the map has no faction home systems of a higher tier.
2553. **Start.** The system of band 0 is the starting system: a basic sector in fringe space. The vanilla `Factions` spawner spreads the factions over the map, so the corporate ally's hot zone (requirement 641) is a blob of one faction's systems.
2554. **Buyer.** On the run map, the Buyer spawns on the first visit to the system of the last band, once per run (in place of the sector rule of requirement 1).
2555. **Sector = step.** On the run map, the sector of the run is its step: 1 + the furthest band that the players entered; the sector count is the map's bands. Core holds both (`Run.Step`, `Run.Steps`, `Run.Progress`); the Journey area fills them through `Run.Hooks`, so other areas need not know the map. An old lane raises it by 2 or 3. The heat panel ("SECTOR 3 / 6"), the overlay, the run log, the run intro, the end screens, the run record, the galaxy map tooltip ("New system: sector 4 of 6"), the stage scaling (requirement 190), the Pursuit ramp (requirement 901), the outage (requirement 920), the hacker (requirement 930) and the relay lockdown roll (requirement 951, which rolls only systems ahead) read it. The sector index (distinct systems visited) still seeds rolls. Another map size uses the sector index and `RunSectorCount` for both. Limit: the events area (`src/HotCargo/Events`) still counts sectors left as `RunSectorCount` − sector index (requirement 83).
2556. **Tests.** Unit tests `RunMapModelTests`: band widths, one start and one Buyer, the band from the X location, 2 ways ahead and a link back for every system, old lanes, tiers, on 1,200 maps of 2 to 10 steps. Autotest `journey-run`: the real map has 6 steps, the lens size, the start in band 0 and the final tier at the Buyer; a second galaxy from a tunables file has 4 steps and final tier 6. Autotest `journey-jump`: on a map of 2 steps, the jump to the system after the start spawns the Buyer.

### The lockdown line

The corporation locks down the map behind the players and closes in on their choices as heat rises. User rule (2026-11-26): the line moves with the players' jumps and their heat, not with time. "Max heat leaves only two options (no less) and minimum heat leaves the most while not allowing back."

2557. **Behind.** Every system of a band before the furthest band that the players entered is closed, at any heat: there is no way back that cuts heat. An old lane closes the bands that it skips. The Buyer's system never closes. `Journey.LineEnabled` turns the line off. The line works only on the run map.
2558. **Choices.** The choices of the players' system are its neighbors that are not behind: side links, links ahead and old lanes. Of n choices, n − 2 may close (none when n is 2 or less). The k-th of them (k = 1 to n − 2) closes at heat 100 × k ÷ (n − 2), so all stay open at heat 0 and exactly two stay open at heat 100. The close order: side links first, then a seeded order (galaxy seed and the two systems), the Buyer last, so it never closes. A choice opens again when heat falls below its closing heat, for example after a paid heat cut. A system switch makes a new set of choices.
2559. **Closed means costly, not blocked.** A closed system stays reachable. An arrival there keeps all heat: a relay jump gets no jump heat cut (requirement 650), and the vanilla switch to a system where own ships wait (requirement 1566, which keeps `Heat.HeatCarryOverFraction` elsewhere) keeps all heat too, so ships parked behind give no cheap way back. The switch checks the closed state when it starts (`GameRoot.JumpToNode`), from sim state.
2560. **Hunters wait.** After every arrival in a closed system, also the recall of a stranded fleet (requirement 1601), the heat stage response `Journey.ClosedWaveStage` (Hunt) of requirement 190 attacks `ClosedWaveDelaySeconds` (6) game seconds after the arrival, always, whatever the heat.
2561. **Warnings.** For each choice that can close, the time until heat reaches its closing heat at the current time heat rate (requirement 24 with its factors; 0 while gains are blocked, requirement 921) gives the warnings: a chat line at `LineFirstWarningSeconds` (300) and at `LineLastWarningSeconds` (60) ("Lockdown: the way to <system> closes in 1:00 (at heat 67)."), and a chat line and a center text when it closes. A choice never closes without the last warning: when heat jumps over its closing heat before that warning (a mission, an event), the last warning comes then, and the choice closes 60 seconds later if heat is still at or above its closing heat. A choice that opens again says so in a chat line, and its warnings start over.
2562. **On the galaxy map.** A closed system shows a red ring and a CLOSED tag under its name. Its tooltip says what closed means and the heat on arrival. An open choice that can close shows its closing heat and the projected time. A red course line marks the line, half a band behind the furthest band that the players entered. Old lanes show in light blue over the vanilla link line.
2563. **Save and determinism.** The run state saves the warning state of each choice and a waiting closed-system wave. The closed state, the choices and the warnings change only in the sim tick and the system switch, from sim state (heat, the map, the visited systems, the galaxy seed), so every co-op machine agrees. The map drawing and the tooltip are UI only; they read one snapshot of the lockdown per sim tick (`Lockdown.UiView`).
2564. **Tests.** Unit tests `LineModelTests`: behind, the closing heats (two left at heat 100), the warning order, the grace after a heat jump, reopening. Autotest `journey-line`: no choice closed at heat 0; a jump to heat 100 closes the closable choices after the grace; heat 0 reopens them; a relay jump into a closed system keeps all heat and queues the hunters.
2565. **Red relay.** In the players' system, the relay (vanilla `FtlGate`) to a closed system shows red: a red ring around it (the white jump pulse ring of the galaxy map, tinted; the vanilla destination frame is green and turns dark when tinted), its screen indicator and its marker on the local system map in red (the vanilla relay color, `FtlGate.IndicatorColor`), and "(CLOSED)" after its name. The relay to a choice under warning (requirement 2561) blinks red, and the warning names that relay. UI only.
2566. **Are you sure.** ENGAGE toward a closed system opens the vanilla jump dialog (requirement 1565), with the line "<system> is closed by the corporate lockdown. The jump cuts no heat (you arrive at heat N), and corporate hunters wait there." and the vanilla question "Jump anyway?". It is one of the dialog lines (`JumpWarnings.JumpLines`), next to the tech that stays (requirement 2522).
2567. **Pay left behind.** On the run map, mission pay that waits at a station or a system here (the active step of a mission is a vanilla `ReceiveRewards` in this system, requirement 2252) is lost with a relay jump away: the arrival removes those missions, like an abandon, so a chain stage gets the result `abandoned`. The ENGAGE dialog (requirement 1565) says so first: "Unpaid missions here: N. Their pay is lost with the jump. Collect it at a station first." The vanilla switch to own ships keeps them. Reason: the run moves forward, and what the players leave behind is gone, like the tech of requirement 2522. Test: `journey-line` (a finished siege, the dialog count, the mission gone after the jump).
