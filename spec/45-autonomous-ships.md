## 45. Autonomous ships

Ships that run only on their ship AI rows (section 30). The player builds them, writes their rows and sets their part settings, but cannot give them orders. Code: `src/HotCargo/Autonomy`, pure rules in `AutonomyModel.cs`. Part rules: `mod/autonomy/`. Strings: `mod/autonomy/strings.en.rules`. Tunables: the `Autonomy` section. The new conditions and actions extend `ShipAiModel` (`src/HotCargo/ShipAi`).

### Terms

- **Autonomous ship**: a human player ship with at least one Computer part and no part of another kind that gives command points (a control room). A ship with a Computer and a control room is a normal ship: the Computer only adds command points and mans its part.
- **Cargo**: the resources in the flexible storage grids of the ship (vanilla storage bays). Ammo, battery and tech storages are not cargo.
- **Loot**: a resource item in space (a vanilla nugget).
- **Trading station**: a ship that trades with the player ship (`ShipAI.WillTradeWith`), a station or a vanilla trade ship.
- **Crewed ship**: a human player ship with a working control room.

### Parts

1050. **Computer**, ID `yarden_zamir.computer`, 2×1, rotatable, `EditorGroup = "Control"`, solid (crew cannot walk through it). It gives command points and sensors like `control_room_small` (`CommandProvider` 50 points; `Sensor` sight 400, radar 1,800). It needs no crew. It needs power: it stores battery and drains 1 power every 60 s like `control_room_small`. It works (is operational) while it has power, its on/off switch is on and it is not scorched. Cost: 16 steel, 40 coil, 4 processors. Health: 2,000 (half of `control_room_small` for half the tiles).
1051. **Computer mans one part.** A working Computer mans exactly one part: the part that touches its front side (the long side that its arrow points to). When two parts touch the front side, the part at the left front tile (seen along the arrow) wins. Only a part with a vanilla crew slot (`PartCrew`) that gives no command points can be manned. The Computer counts as 1 crew member (`AutonomyModel.ComputerCrew`) in the first crew slot (`PartCrew` component) of the manned part. A slot that needs 1 crew is then full; a slot that needs more still needs vanilla crew for the rest, and vanilla crew fill only that rest. The other slots of the part (the manipulator beam has four) need vanilla crew as before. Two Computers can face the same part; it still gets 1 crew member, while one of them works. Code: a postfix on `PartCrew.IsToggleOn` and `IsToggleOnAnticipated` (arrived or assigned crew plus 1 against the slot's crew), a postfix on `PartCrewJob.DesiredCrew` (1 less for the served slot), and the vanilla `ToggleChanged` event from the autonomy tick when a served slot starts, stops, or turns on or off: vanilla raises it only when the full crew arrives.
1052. **The trade-off**: a Computer is 1 crew member for one part, on 2 tiles. Crew quarters with the same 2 tiles hold 2 crew (`crew_quarters_small`), who walk, repair, build, carry power and man any part. So a Computer alone runs only 1-crew parts (a mining laser, a small laser); a ship without crew cannot use parts that need more.
1053. **Targeted reactor**, ID `yarden_zamir.targeted_reactor`, 2×2, not rotatable, `EditorGroup = "Power"`. It makes battery at half the rate of `reactor_small`: 750 battery per second (500 every 2/3 s). It compares to `reactor_small` because both are 2×2: the same tiles give half the power, and in return need no crew and no corridor. It puts the battery straight into the `BatteryStorage` of each part that touches one of its sides, with vanilla `ResourceStorageProxy` components (one per outside edge tile, as `thruster_rocket_battery` does). It never feeds a part that provides power (reactors, power storage), so crew cannot carry its power on. It has no storage that crew can take battery from. It is solid: crew cannot walk through it (`CrewSpeedFactor = 0`, no door places). Overclock and electron debuffs work as on `reactor_small`. Cost: 32 steel, 60 coil, 4 enriched uranium.
1054. **Funnel**, ID `yarden_zamir.funnel`, 2×2, `EditorGroup = "Storage"`. It needs no crew and no power. Every `TickSeconds` of game time (default 0.25 s, on sim ticks that are a multiple of it, so a late co-op client runs it on the same ticks), it takes in each loot item that its filter accepts (requirement 1055) and that lies inside its area: its own tiles grown by `FunnelReach` tiles on each side (default 1, so a 4×4 area). It moves the item into the parts that touch its sides: storage bay tiles and any typed storage tile that accepts the resource and has room (`IResourceSink.GetRemainingCapacity`, minus deliveries that crew already bring). Parts go in order of the touching tile (top, right, bottom, left, each from its start). What finds no room stays in space. Tech crates follow requirement 14: only Tech Vaults take tech. The Funnel is walled and crew can walk through it. Cost: 30 steel, 10 coil.
1055. **Funnel filter**: a vanilla part setting (a popout `UIToggle`, like the missile launcher ammo choice) with the choices: ship loot filter (the default, requirement 1062), all loot, unit value at least 50, at least 200, at least 1,000, off. Unit value is the vanilla sell price of one unit (`GetResourceSellRefund`). Vanilla part settings sync in co-op and save with the ship and the design, so the filter needs no code of its own for that.
1056. **Manipulator beam**: a Computer can man a vanilla manipulator beam. The "collect loot" action (requirement 1068) gives the vanilla collect order for loot in beam range, so the beam pulls loot to the ship from far away. The Funnel is the short-range option without crew and power.
1057. **Art**: each part shows the vanilla deck (`corridor` floor tiles) and a vanilla wall frame by reference from its rules (`mod/autonomy/part_art.rules`), so no game file is copied. Its own layers are in `mod/autonomy/sprites/<part>/`, drawn by `tools/sprites/make_sprites.py` with `tools/sprites/kit.py` (run it with `uv run`; the PNG files are its output, and the script is the source; workflow: `docs/part-art.md`). The own layers are the machine on the deck (layer `turrets`, with damage levels `_33` and `_66`), doodads, dim lights, the roof (green paint mask with holes) with its normal map (green up, like vanilla) and roof glows, the blueprint and the editor icon. The Computer has two server racks and a screen strip at its front, the side it serves; its roof shows the screen glow there. The targeted reactor is a sealed core with a power coupler on each side and an arrow toward each side on its roof; its rules replace the vanilla `reactor_small` visual components by name (`Graphics`, the reactor lights, the battery sprites). The Funnel is a stepped intake with turning vanes and a chute on each side; its roof has a round opening over the intake. Screenshots: `tests/screens/part-art.txt` (each part next to vanilla parts: roofs, build mode, the part list, damage levels).

### Control

1058. **No orders.** An autonomous ship takes no move, attack or weapon target orders from any player. The sim refuses them: `MPEnqueueCommandsInput`, `MPReplaceCommandInput` and the weapon target inputs drop each command or target for an autonomous ship. The UI leaves autonomous ships out of `PlayerInputHandler.SelectedCommandExecutableShips`, so a right click shows no order line and the halt button does not act on them. The player can still select the ship, build on it, change part settings and open the AI editor.
1059. **The ship stays the player's.** An autonomous ship with a working Computer is significant (`SimModeManager.IsShipSignificant`), and losing a part, its crew or a split does not make it a wreck (`CareerSimModeManager.GetAllegianceForReducedShip` keeps the owner). Without a working Computer (destroyed, no power, scorched) the vanilla rules apply again: a ship without crew and command then becomes a wreck on the next loss, as in vanilla.
1060. **Building and repair.** A ship without crew cannot build or repair itself. Crew of an own crewed ship nearby build and repair it with the vanilla helper construction (`SimModeManager.CanShipHelpWithConstruction`). A new autonomous ship starts like any new ship (`AllowStartNewShip`), next to a crewed ship.
1061. **AI.** The rows of section 30 run on autonomous ships the same way. They never wait for a player order (requirement 408), because there are none. A new autonomous ship starts with Auto on and no rows; it holds still until the player adds rows.

### Ship settings

1062. **Cargo settings**: each ship has four lists next to its rows: the loot filter (a minimum unit value, or a list of resource types), the sell list, the mine list (ore types) and the buy list (resource types). An empty sell list sells all cargo. An empty mine list mines any ore. An empty buy list buys nothing. The editor (requirement 410) shows them in a "Cargo" block: a drop list for the loot filter mode and value, and a "Types" button for each list that opens a check list of the resource types. The mine list offers the resources that asteroid deposit parts give (from the asteroid ship rules). The other lists offer every stackable resource except tech.
1063. **Cargo text**: `HCC1:` then `loot=v<value>` or `loot=<id>,<id>`, then `sell=`, `mine=` and `buy=` with resource IDs, joined by `|`. Copy and paste of the editor carry the rows and the cargo text on two lines. An unknown resource ID is refused with a message, like bad rows (requirement 405).
1064. **Return to me**: a switch in the editor's cargo block. Only autonomous ships show it. While it is on, the ship ignores its move rows and follows the owner's first crewed ship (requirement 1071). Target, weapons and facing rows still run. It is the one way to recall an autonomous ship.
1065. The cargo settings and the return switch travel with the rows: the tactics input (requirement 409) carries them, the design and the career save store them (requirement 407), and they are part of the ship's integrity hash.

### New conditions

1066. Values and facts (they extend requirements 2324 and 2325):
   - **My cargo** (percent value): cargo over cargo capacity. "Storage full" is cargo > 95. A ship without storage bays has no value, so every comparison is false.
   - **Cargo to sell** (fact): the cargo holds a resource of the sell list.
   - **Nearest loot** (distance value): the nearest loot item that the loot filter accepts and that the team sees, edge distance from the hull. Loot within the enemy clearance (requirement 1083) does not count.
   - **Station in hail range** (fact): a trading station is within the vanilla trade distance (`CareerSimModeManager.AllowResourceTrade`: hull circles within `LoseHomeShipWarningDistance`, 400). Trade needs this distance, so it stands for "hail distance".
   - **Nearest station** (distance value): the station that "go to station" with "nearest" picks (requirement 1069).
   - **Nearest asteroid** (distance value): an asteroid with ore of the mine list (requirement 1070).
   - **Nearest wreck** (distance value): a wreck (a junk ship that is not an asteroid and has parts) on radar.
   - **Nearest crewed ship** (distance value): the owner's nearest crewed ship, edge to edge; infinite when the owner has no crewed ship in this system.
   - **Lowest hull of the other ships** (percent value, requirement 1083): the lowest hull share of the other human player ships in this system, of any player. The hull share is each part's cost times its health fraction, over the cost of the parts and of the destroyed parts that wait for a rebuild (`ShipAiController.HullHealth`). Parts that a player only added to the blueprint do not count, so a planned build that lacks materials does not hold the looter. Without another ship it is 100.

### New actions

1067. Actions (they extend requirement 402). Move: collect loot, go to station, mine, salvage, return to fleet. A new kind, **Trade**: sell, buy, unload. The first matching trade row runs each AI tick, as with the other kinds.
1068. **Collect loot**: the ship flies so that its nearest Funnel passes over the nearest loot that the loot filter accepts (with no Funnel, its center). It also gives the vanilla collect order (`ManualResourceTransfer`, as requirement 414) for each accepted loot item within the crew safe distance, so crew and manned manipulator beams fetch it. A ship without crew and without a manned manipulator beam gives no collect order, because the open jobs would hold room in the bays that the Funnel needs. It can run while accepted loot is in sight and the cargo has room. Loot within the enemy clearance (requirement 1083) does not count, for this action and for "loot within R". Loot inside the hull circle of another live ship (a station, a trader, an own ship) does not count either: no Funnel can fly over it, and a crewless Looter that picked such a crate waited forever (2026-11-24). Loot on wrecks counts.
1069. **Go to station**, parameter: nearest, best price (pays most for the cargo of the sell list, ties to the nearest) or cheapest (sells the buy list for least, ties to the nearest). The ship flies to the spot `DockDistance` (default 150) from the station hull on its own side, inside the trade distance, and holds there. A station in stasis gets a move order to its place; it turns live as the ship comes near. It can run while the system has a trading station that is not an enemy. A human player ship picks only a station that the players know and that does not refuse them (requirement 657), and only one clear of enemies (requirement 1083). Known: vanilla marks the station discovered (`Landmark.IsDiscovered`, which `Landmark.FixedUpdate` sets once the station is in sight of the human team; it is saved and the same on every co-op machine), or a trade ship that is not a station is on the team radar. Limit: the refusal needs the live station ship, so a station in stasis that refuses counts until it turns live as the ship comes near; the pick then moves on. Scavengers (requirement 1336) pick among every station that trades with them.
1070. **Mine**: the ship flies to the nearest asteroid with ore of the mine list, physical or in stasis. It holds beside its work part: the nearest part that it salvages now, else the nearest part that its order would take. It stands on the part's side of the asteroid (the line from the asteroid center out through the part), so the spot follows the part as the asteroid turns. With a mining laser (a turret with a salvage auto target), the laser with the longest range stands `MineDistance` (default 80) outside the asteroid's bounding circle, closer when its reach (range × `MineReach`, default 0.8) needs it, never inside the circle. The move order's rotation turns the ship so that the center of that laser's firing arc points at the part: the beam leaves the hull forward, inside the arc and clear of the ship's other parts, whatever side of the hull the laser faces. Without a laser, the hull keeps `MineDistance` outside the circle and the ship's +X axis points at the part. A new move order goes out when the spot moves more than a quarter of `MineDistance` or the rotation more than 10 degrees. Limit: the spot is planned for one laser; other lasers fire when the part is in their reach and arc. A part behind another part of the same ship stays blocked (vanilla `IsPathToTargetBlocked`). Within 3 × `MineDistance` it gives the vanilla salvage order (`ResourceManager.SalvagePart`) for the deposit parts with ore of the mine list nearest to the ship, with that list as the collect types, and keeps at most `SalvageJobs` (default 4) such orders open. Vanilla mining lasers pick these jobs (`TurretWeapon.TryAcquireSalvageTarget`); the ore drops as loot, which collect loot and Funnels take in. It can run while such an asteroid exists and the cargo has room. The ore of a deposit part is its `Rules.Resources`. Limit: the mine order uses its own collect list, not the vanilla banned types, because `ShipMetadata.IsResourceTypeBanned` is not deterministic.
1071. **Return to fleet**: the ship follows the owner's first crewed ship (lowest ID). With none in the system it holds still. "Follow my first ship" (requirement 402) and guard "my first ship" use the first crewed ship too when the ship is autonomous.
1072. **Salvage**: like mine, on the nearest wreck on radar, for every part. It can run while a wreck is on radar.
1073. **Sell**: while a trading station is in hail range, the ship sells all cargo of the sell list to the nearest such station. Each unit goes at once from the ship's storage bays to the station's storage (`ResourceManager.AddResources` on the station; only the units it takes leave the bays), and the owner gets the vanilla sell price for it (`GetResourceSellRefund` for the station's faction) through `PlayerState.AddMoney`. An NPC owner (a scavenger, requirement 1336) gets no money. Units that the station has no room for stay. No crew carry anything, so it also works without crew. Limit: delivery missions do not count these units, because vanilla credits a delivery only when crew deliver. Revisit if players use autonomous ships for delivery missions.
1074. **Buy**: the same at once, the other way, from the station in hail range: the ship buys resources of the buy list from the station's stock until the cargo is full, at the vanilla buy cost (`GetResourceBuyCost`), and never spends below `BuyReserve` (default 5,000) of the owner's money.
1075. All new actions read and change only sim state and run in the AI tick (requirement 411). Stations, asteroids and wrecks are picked by distance, ties by object ID. Stasis asteroids come from `sim.Stasis` sorted by distance and reserved ID.

### Scenarios

1076. **Looter** (preset "Looter", requirement 1083): `NearestEnemy<1500 → ReturnToFleet`; `StationInRange & CargoToSell → Sell`; `OthersHealth>90 & Cargo>95 → GoToStation=Nearest`; `OthersHealth>90 & NearestLoot<3000 → CollectLoot`; `OthersHealth>90 & CargoToSell → GoToStation=Nearest`; `Always → ReturnToFleet`. Ship: Computer, Funnel, storage bay, targeted reactor, thrusters. The loot filter picks the loot (value or types), the sell list says what to sell.
1077. **Miner** (preset "Miner"): `Cargo>95 → GoToStation=Nearest`; `StationInRange & CargoToSell → Sell`; `NearestLoot<600 → CollectLoot`; `NearestAsteroid<20000 → Mine`; `Always → ReturnToFleet`. Ship: Computer facing a mining laser, targeted reactor next to both, Funnel, storage bay, thrusters. The mine list picks the asteroid types.
1078. **Supply runner** (preset "Supply runner"): `Cargo>90 → ReturnToFleet`; `Always → GoToStation=Cheapest`; `!NearestFleet>300 → Unload`; `StationInRange → Buy`. It buys the buy list where it is cheapest and unloads all cargo into the owner's nearest crewed ship. **Unload** (trade kind): while an own crewed ship is within the vanilla trade distance, all cargo goes at once into that ship's storages that take it (`ResourceManager.AddResources`). A miner can unload ore for the fleet's factories the same way. A hauler between two stations is not a preset: vanilla buy prices are about twice the sell refund (`EconDifficulty.ResourceSaleRefund` 50 %), and Hot Cargo only raises buy prices (requirement 655), so such a route always loses money.
1079. **Salvager** (preset "Salvager"): `OthersHealth>90 & Cargo>95 → GoToStation=Nearest`; `StationInRange & CargoToSell → Sell`; `OthersHealth>90 & NearestLoot<3000 → CollectLoot`; `OthersHealth>90 & NearestWreck<3000 → Salvage`; `Always → ReturnToFleet`. Like the Looter (requirement 1083), it collects, cuts wrecks and goes to a station only while every other ship's hull is above 90 %, and waits with the fleet until then. It has no flee row. Ship: a Computer facing a mining laser.
1080. **Escort** (preset "Escort drone"): `Health<40 → ReturnToFleet`; `AllyUnderAttack → TargetAllyAttacker`; `NearestEnemy<1500 → TargetNearest`; `Always → Guard=Flagship`. Ship: a Computer facing each weapon it needs (one Computer per weapon), targeted reactors. A guard row of an autonomous ship guards the first crewed ship.
1081. **Return when damaged**: any preset can start with `Health<X → ReturnToFleet`; crew of the crewed ship repair it there (requirement 1060).
1082. Each scenario has an autotest that builds the ship in code from a part list (`auto_build`, no ship files) and runs without teleports after the start: `autonomy-miner`, `autonomy-miner-aim` (a laser that faces -X on a large, turning asteroid), `autonomy-supply`, `autonomy-salvager`, `autonomy-escort`, `autonomy-looter-safety` (requirement 1083: the looter waits while another ship has half its hull, collects after the repair, sells at a known station, and leaves loot near a still enemy alone until the enemy is gone). `autonomy-basic` checks manning, the targeted reactor refill, the refused order, "return to me" and the ownership rules. The co-op session `mp:coop-autonomy` (`tests/autotest-mp/coop-autonomy.txt`, step `autonomy.looter`) runs a looter of the host in its own run, near the start station and its asteroids. The client checks the sale money, the manned laser and the cargo on its own machine, and the session state hash compares both machines. Unit tests cover the model rules: manning pick, Funnel area, filters, cargo text, station and asteroid pick order, sell and buy amounts.

### Loot runner

1083. **Loot runner.** Loot on the ground is often more than the fleet can store. The Looter preset (requirement 1076) collects it and sells it at the nearest known station, but only when it is safe and when no repair needs it:
   - **Wait for the repair.** It collects and goes to a station only while every other ship's hull is above 90 % (`OthersHealth>90`, `ShipAiModel.RepairedPercent`). Until then it waits with the owner's first crewed ship, so the crews keep the loot for the repair. A sale in hail range still runs.
   - **Enemy clearance** (tunable `Autonomy.EnemyClearance`, default 1,500, the engage range of requirement 406; 0 turns it off): a human player ship takes no loot, and picks no station, when the straight path from its center to the loot or the station center, both ends included, comes within this distance of the hull of an enemy on the team radar, hull to hull (`AutonomyModel.PathClear`). This holds for every ship with the actions of requirements 1068 and 1069, not only for the Looter.
   - **Flee.** An enemy closer than 1,500 to the looter (`ShipAiModel.EnemyClearanceDefault`) sends it back to the owner's first crewed ship. There is no other rescue: an enemy can still catch it.
   Saved rows and personal presets keep their text; only the built-in Looter preset changed.

### Build ships

1084. **Designs in the AI settings.** Each ship has `BuildModel.MaxDesigns` (3) design slots next to its cargo settings (requirement 1062). A slot holds a name and a whole ship design in the vanilla design format (`Ship.SaveDesign(Stream)`: a header and the gzip design data, no image), as base64. The editor's "Design k" button opens the vanilla ship library (`ShipLibraryDialog`, load mode); the picked design is copied into the slot. Saved ships are local files of each player, so the copy, not a file path, travels to every co-op machine with the cargo text (requirement 1065). The copy leaves out the design slots of the picked design itself, so slots never nest. It keeps the doors, the part settings and the design's own AI rows and cargo settings.
1085. **Design text**: the cargo text (requirement 1063) gets an optional part `build=<slot>`, slots joined by `,`, each `<name>:<base64>` or empty. A name has at most 32 characters and no `|`, `,`, `:` or line breaks. Text without `build=` has empty slots. A slot whose data does not load as a design, or that has no parts, is refused with a message, like a bad resource ID. The editor refuses a design of another ship class (as build mode does), and a build or refit row does not run for one.
1086. **Design facts and values** (they extend requirements 2324 and 2325):
   - **I can afford a new ship of X** (fact): for each resource type that the design needs, the resources that vanilla construction near this ship can use hold at least that amount. Vanilla counts the storages of the owner's ships within the crew's lose-home warning distance of this ship, crew carried resources and loose resources in sight there (`ResourceManager.GetUsableConstructionResourceTotal`, with anticipated deliveries). The design needs, per type, the sum of its blueprint parts' `ResourcesMinusUnderlying` and its doors' `Resources`, the same amounts that construction takes. Resources that crew could buy at a station do not count.
   - **I can afford a refit to X** (fact): the same, for the cost of the design's blueprint parts and doors that this ship does not have built now (an equal part type at the same tile, rotation and flip; a door of the same type at the same place). Resources that deconstruction of the old parts gives back do not count: vanilla drops them only while crew take the parts apart.
   - **Cost of design X in T** and **refit cost of design X in T** (amount values): the amounts above for one resource, to compare with "resource T on board" or a number.
   - **Ships of design X** (count value): the owner's live ships in this system that a "Build ship" row started from that design (requirement 1088), finished or not. "Fewer than 3" is `ShipsOf[1]<3`.
1087. **Build ship X** (an action of the build kind, requirement 403: the first matching build row runs, each tick, before the trade row). The ship starts the design as a new ship of its owner, the way a player does: a new empty ship (as `MPNewShipInput`) where `CareerSimModeManager.AllowStartNewShip` allows it, then the design's blueprint parts and doors (as build mode "Load ship (replace)", `ShipUpdateInfo.CopyBlueprintsFromShip`), then build mode's "Make it so" (`ShipUpdateInfo.TryConstruct` with its flags and no crew to hire, then the queued crew construction). When vanilla says that construction is blocked, the blueprints stay and the log says so. Crew of nearby own ships build it with vanilla helper construction (requirement 1060). The spot is the first of 8 directions around this ship, starting toward the owner's nearest crewed ship (whose crew build it; without one, from +X), where the new ship's circle touches no other ship, at this ship's radius plus the design's radius plus `Autonomy.BuildGap` (100), and that `AllowStartNewShip` allows: in team sight, within the crew's lose-home distance of an own ship, no enemy within the vanilla no-build radius. Crew must also reach the spot: an own crewed ship lies within the crew's lose-home warning distance (400) of the new ship, its construction assist allows the help (`CanShipHelpWithConstruction`) and it is slow enough for crew to leave (`AllowAutoCrewExit`): vanilla's construction helper rule (`Job.GetNearbyConstructionHelperShips`). Without such a spot the row does not run (requirement 403), so a build never leaves a blueprint that nobody builds. Vanilla charges no money for a new ship; construction takes resources only. The new ship gets the design's AI rows, Auto switch and cargo settings, and the name of the design. It can run while the design's slot is filled, the construction mode is crew construction (`GetConstructionMode`), a spot exists, and no earlier build of this ship is unfinished (requirement 1088).
1088. **One build at a time.** A ship remembers the ship that its last build started (sim state, saved, part of the integrity hash). While that ship lives and its construction is not done (its parts and doors do not match its blueprints), "Build ship" does not run. Each started ship remembers its design (a stable hash of the design data), for the value "ships of design X". Vanilla career has no fleet cap (the game setup's ship count only limits the starting ships, `CareerMPModeConfigGui.GetMaxShipsForPlayer`), so a row needs this condition to stop.
1089. Co-op: the action runs in the AI tick on every machine with the same design data from the cargo text, and the new ship comes from sim state only. Tests: `autonomy-build` (an autonomous ship collects the resources that the fleet lacks, "Can afford" turns true, one ship starts and its construction finishes, no second one starts, then a refit row rebuilds a ship into another design once) and the co-op step `autonomy.build` (the same build on both machines under the state hash). Unit tests cover the design text, the slot parameter text, the design conditions and the afford rule.
1090. **Refit to design X** (an action of the build kind). The ship's blueprints become the design's, as build mode "Load ship (replace)" does (requirement 407): the blueprints are cleared and the design's parts and doors are added (`ShipUpdateInfo.CopyBlueprintsFromShip` steps), then "Make it so" queues the construction (requirement 1087). Crew take the old parts apart and build the new ones the vanilla way. The ship keeps its own AI rows, cargo settings and design slots: those rows started the refit and are the player's plan for this ship. A player who wants the design's rows uses "Load ship (replace)" in build mode, which brings them (requirement 407). It can run while the slot is filled, the design has the ship's ship rules, the construction mode is crew construction, construction is legal for the ship (`Ship.IsConstructionLegal`: not in combat), and the ship's blueprints are not already the design's, so a refit row runs once per design. The AI spends no money: unlike a player's build, it does not buy missing resources at a station.

Requirements 1091 to 1099 stay free for this section.
