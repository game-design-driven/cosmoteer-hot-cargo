## 30. Player ship tactics

Dragon Age style tactics for the players' own ships. Code: `src/HotCargo/ShipAi`. Pure model: `ShipAiModel.cs`. Strings: `mod/shipai/strings.en.rules`. Tunables: the `ShipAi` section.

400. Each player ship can have a tactics list: up to 16 rows of "conditions → action". A row has 1 or more condition terms, with no upper limit (requirement 416), and brackets may group them (requirement 2320). A ship without rows has no tactics state.
401. **Terms**: a condition term is a fact (requirement 2324) or a comparison of a value (requirement 2323). Every fact, value and action is one entry of the registry `ShipAiRegistry` (requirement 2325): the text form, the editor lists and the tooltips read only it.
402. **Actions** have seven kinds. Target: nearest, weakest (lowest health), strongest (highest blueprint value), the enemy that attacks an ally, the enemy that attacks this ship, the enemy that attacks a ship with a Tech Vault (this ship, or an ally within `AllyHelpRadius` as for the ally fact). A target row whose terms are all joined by AND and include "nearest enemy < R" (without NOT, R a number) picks only among the enemies within the smallest such R, with the anti-flicker margin of requirement 418 (`ShipAiModel.EngageRange`); other rows pick among every enemy on the team radar. Move: keep at range (the target at `KiteRangeFraction` of the longest weapon range, edge to edge), close in (to `CloseInDistance`), hold position, retreat to the nearest ally, retreat and repair (move away from the nearest enemy until `RetreatDistance` away; the crew repair as usual), flee and jump (a local FTL jump to the friendly ship furthest from the nearest enemy when the vanilla jump rules allow it, else flee on thrusters), follow my first ship, guard (my first ship, the vault ship or the weakest ally: follow it and attack the enemy that attacks it), collect tech crates (requirement 414), and the cargo actions of requirement 1067 (collect loot, go to station, mine, salvage, return to fleet). Facing: requirement 415. Fire, for a weapon group (all weapons or part control group 1 to 10): at will, focus (the part that the vanilla AI values most), at their weapons, at their engines (the nearest such part), point defense only, hold fire, disruptors (requirement 414). Trade: sell, buy, unload (requirement 1067). Build: build ship and refit to design (requirements 1087 and 1090). Flag: set flag N, clear flag N (requirement 2326). Ties go to the nearest ship, then to the sim order.
403. With `Auto` on, the ship evaluates its rows every `TickSeconds` (default 1 s) of game time. Rows run from top to bottom. A row matches when its terms hold (requirement 416) and its action can run now (a target action needs that target, keep at range and close in need an enemy; retreat to an ally, follow and guard need that ship). The first matching target row picks the target, the first matching move row picks the movement, the first matching facing row picks the facing and the first matching trade row trades (requirement 1067) and the first matching build row builds (requirement 1087). The build row runs before the trade row, so a sale in the same tick cannot take resources that "can afford" counted. Every matching flag row runs, in row order, after the evaluation, so a flag change shows at the next tick. With a target and no move row, the ship attacks it with the vanilla attack order.
404. Each weapon follows the first matching Fire row of its group or of all weapons (a disruptors row only for disruptor weapons, requirement 414), else fires at will. Hold fire and point defense only take away the fire permission (point defense weapons keep it under point defense only). Focus, weapons and engines give the weapon a part target on the tactics target (disruptors: requirement 414); point defense keeps its own targets. A part target that the player set stays.
405. The tactics text form is `HC2:` and rows joined by `;`, each `terms->Action[=parameter]` (terms: requirement 417). The arrow is `->`, because `>` is a comparison. A number parameter is a whole number; a parameter of a list is the entry's name (`Face=BroadsideLeft`, `GoToStation=BestPrice`, `Guard=VaultShip`). Copy and paste use it. Bad text (an unknown name, a missing, wrong or out-of-range parameter, a comparison of two values of different units, more than 16 rows, or brackets that do not balance) is refused with a message and changes nothing. Text of the old form (`HC1:`) is refused like bad text: there is no migration, so old ship designs, saves and personal presets with rows load without them (requirement 407).
406. Presets are starting rows that the player can change: Aggressive, Defensive, Sniper, Escort, Vault Guard (`ShipAiModel.PresetRows`). Aggressive and Sniper target and move only while the nearest enemy is closer than 1500, edge to edge (`ShipAiModel.EngageRangeDefault`: about twice the longest weapon range, the missile launcher's 700, and below the start ship's radar of 1807; the range of the Defensive, Formation and Escort Drone rows), and end with "Always → follow my first ship", so a second ship stays with the fleet while no enemy is that close. Escort targets the enemy that attacks the escort before it guards. The player's own presets follow them in the list (requirement 422).
407. The rows and the Auto switch are saved with the ship design: `Ship.SaveDesign` writes the nodes `HotCargoTactics`, `HotCargoTacticsAuto` and, with cargo settings, `HotCargoTacticsCargo` (requirement 1062), and `Ship.Load` reads them. The game reads design nodes by name, so a design with tactics loads without the mod (without tactics). The build menu's "Load ship (replace)" copies the tactics of the design to the live ship through the tactics input; a design without tactics keeps the ship's tactics. The live ship's full tactics state is saved in the career save, as the path `HotCargoTactics` of the ship. A co-op join and a resync carry it the same way. A design or a save with bad tactics text loads without tactics and shows the error.
408. Player orders come first. While the ship has a player order (a command with a player ID) that is not done, the tactics wait and drop their own order and part targets. A move order is done when the ship arrives. The tactics remove only the order that they gave. Auto off, `ShipAi.Enabled` off, or a ship that is no longer a human ship drops the decision the same way. The rows stay.
409. Every edit (the editor, a paste, a preset, a design load, apply to selected) sends the whole row list, the Auto switch, the cargo settings and the return switch (requirement 1065) as the MP input `HotCargoShipTactics`. Only a player who can command the ship can change it. The editor shows the sim's rows, so an edit by another player shows in the open window.
410. **Editor**: Ctrl+K, or the AI button in the ships card, opens a window for the first selected own ship (Ctrl+K with no selection: the first own ship). The card shows the button for one selected own ship in its info row, and for several selected own ships at its right edge, because vanilla hides the info row then (`ShipsCard.UpdateForMultipleShips`). It is a vanilla command button of the card (`ShipGui.SHIP_COMMAND_BUTTON`, 50 x 50, as the halt button) with an icon: green when every selected own ship has Auto on, HUD blue when none has, half green and half blue when some have (`ShipAiModel.CardStateOf`). Its tooltip names the state ("ON in n of m ships" when some have), the preset of the ships (a built-in one first, then a personal one with the same rows, else "own rows"; `ShipAiModel.PresetOf`), what a click does, and Ctrl+K. The window holds, top to bottom: the Auto switch, the preset list with Apply and Delete, Copy, Paste, Clear; the preset name with Save as preset and Apply to selected; the cargo block and the design slots (requirements 1062, 1064, 1084); a status line (off, waiting for a player order; else on or autonomous, the current target, the move row in effect with its number and action, the flags that are set, or that no row matches); one block per row (requirement 419); Add row; and a short help text. The number of each row in effect now is green. The preset list shows the preset that the rows equal, if any. The window scrolls. The window uses the vanilla window, drop list and button styles. Autotests never use the system clipboard.
411. Determinism: the tactics run in the sim tick (`EventHub.Tick`), walk the ships in sim order, and read only sim state. They give vanilla commands with sim command IDs. The tactics state is part of the ship's integrity hash. They also run after a win, because the career goes on.
412. Limit: the tactics control only player ships; they are not a vanilla `ShipAI`. A vanilla AI on a player ship would change comms, stasis and ship splits. Revisit if the game adds an autopilot for player ships.

### Hot Cargo hooks, condition chains, fleet tools and facing

413. **Hot Cargo facts and values** read other areas through their public state. Values: heat stage (`RunState.StagesStarted`), seconds to the Strike wave (`Run.SecondsToStrikeWave`: only at full heat in the last stage; no value before), distance to the nearest tech crate (tech nuggets that the team sees, edge distance from the hull). Facts: hunters in the system (`HunterSpawner.Hunters`), the tech loss timer runs (`LossTimer.IsRunning`), an enemy holds our tech (an enemy on radar with an entry in `TechState.HunterHeld`), an enemy calls for help (an enemy on radar whose `CallStrategy.Calling` is true).
414. **Hot Cargo actions**. Target: the enemy that holds our tech, the enemy that calls for help (nearest first). Move: collect tech crates: the ship flies to the nearest tech crate on radar until its hull is `CollectDistance` away, and gives the vanilla crew collect order (`ResourceManager.ManualResourceTransfer`, as `SalvageJob.OnPartSalvaged` does) for each tech crate within the crew's safe distance (`Crew.LoseHomeShipWarningDistance`, the limit of the vanilla collect tool). It can run while a crate is on radar and the ship has room for tech. Fire: disruptors: only disruptor weapons follow this row; other weapons skip it and follow the next matching Fire row. A disruptor weapon is a weapon of a `DisruptorParts` part or of a part that stores `DisruptorAmmo` now (a missile launcher set to EMP missiles). It aims at the part that the vanilla AI values most on the nearest enemy that calls for help, else on the tactics target.
415. **Facing**: an action of its own kind with a side: nose, left side, right side, stern. The first matching facing row turns the tactics attack order with the vanilla rotation options: `RotationTarget` = the target, `RotationMode` = follow angle relative, `Rotation` = 0°, +90°, −90° or 180° from the nose (`BaseFollowCommand.GetWorldRotation`), less the design's `Ship.FlightDirection`, because the nose points at the ship rotation plus that direction. It can run while an enemy is on radar. Without a facing row, the attack order uses the vanilla best attack rotation. A change of the facing row gives a new attack order.
416. **Condition chains**: each term has its body (a fact with its parameter, or a comparison), a NOT switch and a connector to the term before it, AND or OR. AND binds tighter than OR: "A AND B OR C AND D" means (A and B) or (C and D). Brackets change the order (requirement 2320). Every term is evaluated on every tick, so each one keeps its memory (requirement 418).
417. **Chain text**: terms are joined by `&` (AND) or `|` (OR). A fact is `[!]Name[=parameter]`. A comparison is `[!]Value[arguments]` then `<`, `>` or `=`, then a whole number or another value with its arguments: `Health<20`, `NearestEnemy<1500`, `Resource[steel]<Cost[1,steel]`, `ShipsOf[1]<3`. Arguments stand in square brackets, joined by `,`: a resource ID, a design slot, or both (slot first). Example: `HC2:!CarryingTech&Health<40|HeatStage>2->CollectTech`.
418. **Anti-flicker**: a comparison of a percent, distance or seconds value that holds keeps holding until the value passes the other side by `HysteresisMargin` (percent: points × 100; distance and seconds: a fraction of the other side). Such a term keeps a new result at least `MinHoldSeconds` of game time. `=` gets no margin, and comparisons of counts, money, amounts and stages get none: they change in whole steps. NOT applies after both. Facts follow at once. A value that the ship does not have (shields without shield parts, the Strike wave outside the last stage, a slot without a design) makes every comparison false. The term memory (result and game second of each term) is sim state: it is saved with the ship, is part of its integrity hash, and resets when the rows change or the decision drops (requirement 408).
419. **Editor rows**: a row block has one line per term: IF (first term) or an AND/OR switch, the opening brackets, a NOT switch, the group list (always, my ship, enemies, my fleet, nearby, the run, cargo, flags) and the entry list of that group (its facts, then its values, from the registry; the tooltip describes the selected entry; a group with one entry shows no entry list), the parameter of a fact or the argument list of a value (a resource, a design slot, or both together), and for a value the comparison button (click: `<`, `>`, `=` in turn) and the other side: a number list, or a value list after the "value" button switches it (only values of the same unit, with arguments filled in from the ship's design slots and the left side's resource; the "number" button switches back; each button's tooltip says what it does). Then the closing brackets, "+" (a new term after it, in the same brackets), the bracket buttons of requirement 2321, and a remove button while the row has more than one term. A last line, THEN, holds the action group list (target, move, fire, face, trade, build, flags), the action list of that group, its parameter, "+ AND" (a new term at the end, outside all brackets), up, down and remove. Each row block has the vanilla box frame, with a small gap between rows. The minimum window is 1,300 wide; at it the condition and action lists fit the longest built-in name on one line in the game font, and the other lists their longest value. `tactics_editor fit` checks it in a game with graphics; a headless game has no glyph metrics and only logs that.
420. Every term edit (add, insert, remove, NOT, AND/OR, condition, argument, comparison, other side, bracket) sends the whole row list as one tactics input (requirement 409).
2320. **Brackets**: terms can stand in brackets, nested to any depth. A bracket is evaluated like a row (AND before OR) and then counts as one term. In the text form, `(` stands before a term and its NOT, `)` after it: `HC2:Health<40&(NearestEnemy<1000|!HuntersInSystem)->RetreatRepair`. The AND or OR in front of a bracket joins the whole bracket. A bracket has no NOT of its own: NOT (A AND B) is NOT A OR NOT B.
2321. **Bracket buttons**: "+( )" on a term line puts that term in a new bracket; "+" on a term inside it adds terms to the bracket. "-( )" on a term that opens a bracket removes that bracket and its closing bracket; the terms stay. Removing a term removes the brackets around only that term and keeps the others balanced (`ShipAiModel.RemoveTerm`). A term line shows its opening brackets before its NOT switch and its closing brackets after the parameter, and is indented one step for each open bracket around it, up to 4 steps. Unit tests: `ShipAiModelTests` (evaluation, text, edits). Game test: `shipai-hotcargo` (bracket text through the tactics input). Screenshot: `tests/screens/shipai.txt` (`shipai-editor-brackets`).
421. **Apply to selected**: the button sends this window's rows and Auto switch to every other selected ship that the local player commands, one tactics input per ship. The input checks the permission for each ship (requirement 409). The button shows the number of those ships and is off with none.
2322. **Design and resource arguments**: "Build ship", "Refit to design" and the facts "I can afford a new ship of X" and "I can afford a refit to X" take a design slot of the ship (1 to `BuildModel.MaxDesigns`), shown with the slot's design name. The values "ships of design X", "cost of design X in T" and "refit cost of design X in T" take a slot (and a resource), "resource T on board" takes a resource (a stackable resource that is not tech, as the cargo lists). A row that names an empty slot is refused with a message (requirement 405), in the tactics input and in a paste.
2323. **Comparison**: a value, then `<`, `>` or `=`, then a whole number or a second value of the same unit. Units: percent (0 to 100), distance (world units, edge to edge from this ship's hull), seconds, count, stage, money, amount (resource units). Percent values are fractions shown × 100. A distance to something that does not exist is infinite: "nearest enemy < 1500" is false without an enemy, "nearest enemy > 1500" true. `<=`, `>=` and `!=` are not offered: NOT gives them (NOT `>` is `<=`, NOT `=` is `!=`). Each unit has number choices in the editor; a pasted other number shows too.
2324. **Facts** (yes or no, some with a parameter): always, I lost a part of a kind (weapon, thruster, shield, Tech Vault; a destroyed part that is not rebuilt yet), an enemy targets me, an ally is under attack (an enemy has an attack order on another human ship within `AllyHelpRadius` (default 2000) of this ship, edge to edge, or of its guard target when it has a Guard row), an enemy of a class is on radar (smaller or larger by blueprint value, or a station: a ship without thrusters), I carry Stolen Tech, my Tech Vault is damaged, the Hot Cargo facts of requirement 413, the cargo facts of requirement 1066, an ally is in my line of fire (requirement 424), the design facts of requirement 1086, and flag N is set (requirement 2326).
2325. **Values** (`ShipAiRegistry`, one entry each with its unit, argument kind, group and number choices): my health (as requirement 47), my shields (shield charge over capacity; none without shields), my ammo (the supplies that parts burn, as UX.md V7, without battery, hyperium and tech), my battery, my cargo (requirement 1066), the lowest hull of the other ships (requirement 1083), the distances to the nearest enemy (on the team radar), ally, own crewed ship, loot, trading station, asteroid, wreck and tech crate (requirements 413, 1066), enemies on radar, my fleet size (the owner's ships in this system, this ship included), heat stage, seconds to the Strike wave, money (the owner's), resource T on board (this ship's storages), cost of design X in T and refit cost of design X in T (requirement 1086), and ships of design X (requirement 1088). A row reads only the values that its terms name, once per tick.
2326. **Flags**: each ship has `ShipAiModel.MaxFlags` (4) flags, all clear when the rows change. "Set flag N" and "clear flag N" are actions of the flag kind; "flag N is set" is a fact. Flags give simple modes, for example a looter that sets flag 1 when its cargo is full and goes to sell while flag 1 is set, and clears it when its cargo is empty. The flags are sim state: saved with the ship and part of its integrity hash. Limit: flags have numbers, not names, because a name needs a text field per term; revisit if players keep more than a few modes.
422. **Personal presets**: Save as preset stores the current rows under the typed name (at most 32 characters, no line breaks) in the local file `hot_cargo_tactics_presets.txt` next to the game settings (`Paths.RootSavePath`), one "name TAB text" line each. A preset with the same name is replaced. The list shows them after the built-ins as "Mine: name"; Delete removes the selected one. The file is a player preference, not sim state: each player has their own. A bad line is skipped. Applying a personal preset is an edit like any other (requirement 409).


### Formations

Ships of one player that fly together keep apart, keep a shape and do not shoot through each other. Pure rules: `FormationModel.cs`. Vanilla pathfinding and collision avoidance still steer the ships; the rows only give vanilla orders.

423. **Nearest ally**: a distance value: the nearest friendly human ship (any player), edge to edge. Hysteresis as requirement 418.
424. **An ally is in my line of fire**: a condition: the hull circle of a friendly human ship crosses the straight line from this ship's center to its attack target's center (the target of its current attack order, else the nearest enemy on radar), between the two (`FormationModel.BlocksLine`). With "Fire: hold" it stops friendly fire: "IF an ally is in my line of fire → Fire: hold".
425. **Move: hold formation slot**, parameter: the spacing. The ship holds a slot of a V behind its leader (its player's first ship, requirement 402; an autonomous ship: the first crewed ship, requirement 1071) with the vanilla follow order (`FollowCommand` with `FollowAngle` relative to the leader, `EdgeToEdgeFollowRadius`, facing where the leader faces). Slot 0 is on the left arm, slot 1 on the right arm, then one rank further out each (`FormationModel.SlotOf`, arms at 150 degrees from the leader's nose, rank × spacing). Slots go to the ships of the player that have a formation row, by ship ID, without the leader, so every co-op machine gives the same slots. It can run while the ship has a leader.
426. **Move: keep spacing from allies**, parameter: the spacing. While the nearest friendly human ship is nearer than the spacing (edge to edge), the ship moves straight away from it to the spacing plus 50 (`FormationModel.SpacingTarget`). It runs only then, so a keep-spacing row above other move rows stops the ships from ramming each other and lets the rows below steer otherwise.
427. **Preset "Formation"**: `AllyInLineOfFire → HoldFire`; `Health < 25 → RetreatToAlly`; `NearestEnemy < 1500 → TargetNearest`; `Always → KeepSpacing=150`; `Always → HoldFormation=300`. The autotest `shipai-formation` checks the slots, the spacing and the held fire.
