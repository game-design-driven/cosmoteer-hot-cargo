## 50. Debris decay

A long fight leaves the system full of loose junk, and the game simulates every live piece on every machine. Late-game lag and salvage chores are frequent complaints in reviews. So old loose junk with no value glows and then burns up. Salvage was also most of the players' wealth in the playtest of 14 and 15 November, so broken parts drop less, and moving on to the next mission pays more (BALANCE.md, finding 11). Code: `src/HotCargo/Debris/`. Numbers: the `Debris` tunables section. Requirements 1240 to 1249.

1240. **Decay.** A junk piece (a ship with junk allegiance, `Ship.IsJunk`) that can decay (requirement 1241) waits, glows (requirement 1243) and then burns up: the game removes it. `Debris.Enabled` (default on) turns the decay off; then junk stays, as in vanilla, and nothing is tracked.
1241. **What stays.** A piece never decays when it is not junk, is an asteroid, has more than `MaxParts` (20) parts (a wreck worth salvage), holds any resource (stored, carried by crew or in transit: tech, mission items, cargo), has crew aboard, is a station or landmark (a special ship component), is the relic (`hc_relic_ship`) or a vanilla abandoned ship (label `abandoned`), is named by a mission (`Mission.IsShipAssociated`), has a part that crew salvage or that lost health to salvage, is in reach of a player's tractor beam that pulls something (the beam's stretch range plus `TractorMargin`, 100), or is locked against removal (`AllowCullShip`). Tech crates and mission item crates are not ships, so they never decay. Limit: a piece is safe only while a beam pulls, not before; the glow warns first.
1242. **Age.** A piece starts to age at the first check at which it can decay. It burns up `DecaySeconds` (240) game seconds later. A piece that becomes protected, goes into stasis or leaves with a jump starts from zero when it can decay again. Limit: junk in stasis never decays, but the game does not simulate it either.
1243. **Glow.** In its last `GlowSeconds` (60) a piece shows a pulsing orange outline on every part. The outline gets brighter and pulses faster toward the burn-up. The glow is drawn from the run state and never changes the sim. Screenshot script: `tests/screens/debris.txt`.
1244. **Check.** The check runs in the sim tick, every `CheckSeconds` (2) game seconds (a whole number of ticks, at least 1). It walks the live ships in sim order.
1245. **Co-op.** The decay reads sim state only (run game seconds, sim tick, ships, missions, jobs, beams) and changes it only in the sim tick. The ages are run state (`RunState.Debris`), so a save, a load and a co-op resync keep them, and the co-op state hash compares them. The removal skips the game's lost-ship saver, because that reads the local player and writes a local file. So every machine removes the same pieces at the same tick. The state hash of every co-op session compares the debris ages, and `debris-decay` checks the decay itself and that the ages survive a save and load.
1246. **Tests.** `debris-decay` (single player): small pieces glow and burn up, a big piece and tech crates stay, and the switch off keeps new junk. Unit tests: `DebrisModelTests`.
1247. **Salvage drop.** When a part breaks on a ship that the players did not build, its chance to drop each unit of a cheap resource loose is × `SalvageDropFactor` (0.6), in every tier; expensive components get a milder cut (requirement 1832). A break by salvage damage (a salvage or mining laser) follows requirement 1834 instead, for its salvage share. A break is a part removal without a queued deconstruction: weapons, salvage damage, collisions, explosions. Not affected: asteroids (requirement 1248), a deconstruction and its refund, the contents of a broken storage, parts that float free when a ship splits, and parts that the players built. A station that rebuilds from debris (spec 22, requirement 96) takes the debris parts in whole; they do not break, so they drop nothing either way. The station guns that raiders break first (requirement 91) are broken parts, so the factor applies to them. The factor applies after the economy drop factor and its cap at 1, so the game rolls each unit once, as before.
1248. **Ore yield.** When an asteroid part breaks, its ore × `MiningYieldFactor` (1.2) for a break by weapons or a collision, and × `LaserMiningYieldFactor` (1.3) for a break by salvage damage (a mining laser). A part with both kinds of damage gets a mix by its salvage share. The game tells the two apart: a laser break drops ore at 100 %, a weapon break at 10 %. So the laser factor is the whole bonus, not 1.2 × 1.3. The factor scales the quantity, because a chance cannot go above 100 %: the whole part of quantity × factor drops, and the fraction gives one more unit with that chance. That roll comes from a hash of the asteroid and the part's place, not from the sim's random rolls.
1249. **Tests.** `debris-salvage` (single player): with `SalvageDropFactor` 0 broken junk drops nothing, and with 1 it drops; large control rooms that weapons break drop only processors with the base cut at 0 and no processor with the component cut at 0 (requirement 1832); a laser break of 5 large control rooms drops exactly 45 of their 50 processors and, with `LaserBaseDropFactor` 0, no steel (requirement 1834); a bay of 40 bullets drops none when weapons break it, none when a salvage beam cuts it, and 40 at a `LaserBayAmmoShare` of 1 (requirement 1835); a 2×2 bay holds 320 steel (requirement 2410); a laser break of 10 iron deposits drops exactly 20 iron at a laser factor of 2, 10 at 1, and 10 to 20 at 1.3. Unit tests: `DebrisModelTests` (`ScaleQuantity`, `ByLaserShare`, `SalvageFactor`, `LaserSplit`). Limit: no test covers a deconstruction refund (the code leaves it out by the queued-deconstruction check). Revisit when a refund changes.
1832. **Components drop a bit less** (user, 2026-11-23: "expensive component types to only reduce a bit"). In the salvage cut of requirement 1247 (weapon breaks; laser breaks: requirement 1834), a resource whose base price (`BuyPrice` in its rules) is at least `SalvageComponentPrice` (1,000) is an expensive component: its drop chance is × `SalvageComponentDropFactor` (0.85). Every cheaper resource keeps `SalvageDropFactor`. The game's prices have a gap of 4 times there (gold 500, enriched uranium 2,000), so with the defaults the components are enriched uranium, processor and diamond; every other resource, also tristeel (200) and coil2 (300), keeps 0.6. Stolen tech (10,000) is above the price, but it never drops as a part's resource (it drops from vault storage, requirement 445). `debris_split` logs the split of every loaded resource. Unit test: `DebrisModelTests` (`SalvageFactor`); game test: `debris-salvage`.
2370. **Cargo comes back in full** (user rule, 2026-11-23: "Reduce is only for parts to crates ratio, not crates to crates"). When a storage bay tile breaks, on any ship (thieves, pirates, hunters, scavengers, players), its contents drop 1:1 as loose resources; vanilla drops half. Resources that a killed crew member carries drop 1:1 too, if they are cargo (requirement 1833). Ammo keeps the vanilla chance (requirement 1835). Only parts that turn into crates get the cut (requirement 1247). Not changed: tech, which keeps its own rules (requirements 65, 445, 1549), and part internals that are not cargo (batteries, ammo, factory and typed buffers). Test: `debris-salvage` (a broken storage bay with 320 steel drops exactly 320).
1833. **No loose non-cargo.** A resource without a stack size (`MaxStackSize` 0 in its rules: vanilla battery, fire extinguisher, heat, ion energy) is no cargo, and requirement 2370 leaves its drop chance as vanilla has it: 0. Reason: a killed crew member carried battery power, it dropped as a loose battery, and the vanilla collect tool divided by its stack size (`ResourceManager.GetCapacityTotal`) and crashed a playtest host (2026-11-23). Such a loose resource is also invisible. Each sim tick removes loose non-cargo that a save from an older build still holds. Test: `debris-collect` (the collect tool's plan over a crate of every resource that can lie loose, next to a steel crate; an old loose battery goes).
1834. **Laser breaks drop 90 %** (user, 2026-11-24: "things that break from mining laser drop 90% of their original parts no matter what, not including steel and coils which get a slight increase instead"). When salvage damage (a salvage or mining laser) breaks a part on a ship that the players did not build, the part's resources split by its salvage share. The laser share drops `LaserDropShare` (0.9) for sure, with no roll and no cut: 10 processors drop 9. The rest drops as a weapon break (requirement 1247, 1832). The resources in `LaserBaseResources` (steel, coil) keep the roll instead: their chance is × `LaserBaseDropFactor` (0.75) for the salvage share and × `SalvageDropFactor` for the rest. On the "fast" economy a laser-cut steel unit drops at 24 % (was 19.2 %) and a coil unit at 18 % (was 14.4 %): a slight increase, because steel and coil are the bulk of the loot. The rounding rolls come from a hash of the place and the resource, not from the sim's random rolls. Not affected: asteroids (requirement 1248), parts that the players built, tech, and no-cargo resources. Measured with `econ_loot` (4 pirate ships, salvage beams, before → after): tier 1 steel 229 → 279, coil 155 → 187, coil2 5 → 21, enriched uranium 6 → 29, processors 3 → 7; tier 3 steel 515 → 676, coil 214 → 253, coil2 8 → 46, enriched uranium 8 → 28, processors 3 → 6. Weapon breaks do not change.
1835. **No bay ammo.** Requirement 2370 does not raise a resource that vanilla never drops from a bay that weapons break (`StorageDestroyed` 0 %: the ammo types). A weapon break of a bay drops none of it, a killed crew member none, and a salvage beam only `LaserBayAmmoShare` (0) of the vanilla chance (vanilla `StorageSalvaged` 100 %: all of it) (user, 2026-11-24: laser salvage stays rich in parts, without the bullet flood). Ammo in a part's own buffers (a gun's magazine) stays vanilla. The mod's own items (IDs in its namespace, `yarden_zamir.`) keep the rates of their rules: the Tracer Beacon has `StorageDestroyed` 0 % so that shots destroy it, and a salvage beam frees it (test `events-abandoned`). Measured, 4 tier-3 pirates: broken by weapons, 440 bullets before #277 and 0 after; cut by salvage beams, 664 bullets before and 224 after (the bays held 440; the rest came from the guns' magazines).
